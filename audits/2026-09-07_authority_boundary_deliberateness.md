# Authority Boundary Deliberateness

**Question posed (relayed from an external ChatGPT consultation, follow-up to `audits/2026-09-07_consequence_authority_map.md`):** Where are the authority boundaries deliberately established, and where are they accidental?

## Executive Answer

Every AUTHORITATIVE connection found in this codebase traces to a specific, dated Finding or commit where a human or session deliberately chose to write the call. Where authority is *absent*, the split is real and roughly even: two cases are deliberate non-decisions (Shadow's correction, tonight's attempt ledger — both explicitly built or left inert on purpose, with a stated reason), and two are genuinely accidental — one is pure, undecided absence (`retrieve_relevant_memories` sitting unused in `self_edit_manager.py` since the project's very first commit, never touched since), and one is dated, provable collateral damage (a garden duplicate-filter that predates `seam_engine`'s existence by six days, and therefore could not possibly have been written with seam attribution in mind, yet silently discards 76 of its 78 first-ever detections anyway).

## The Accidental Cases, Compared

The two accidental cases look superficially similar (both leave a real producer's output stranded) but have opposite implications for what closing them would require:

- **Pure absence (`retrieve_relevant_memories`)** — nobody ever decided anything, for or against. Closing this gap is purely additive: write the missing call. There is no prior decision to reconcile, override, or ask "was there a reason this was left out" about — there wasn't one.
- **Collateral damage (`seam_engine`)** — a real, deliberate decision exists and is still doing exactly what it was built to do (stopping literal near-duplicate garden spam). Closing this gap is not purely additive — it means either carving out an exemption inside someone else's working, load-bearing filter, or building a second, seam-aware persistence path alongside it. The fix has to coexist with a decision that's still correct for the thing it was actually built to solve.

This distinction is the sharpest new finding from this pass: "no evidence of a decision" and "evidence of a decision about something else" are not the same failure mode, and conflating them (as a first read of either case might) would lead to the wrong kind of fix being proposed for one or the other.

## What This Implies, in General

FeralEcho's authority boundaries are **deliberate where authority is granted, and split roughly evenly between deliberate and accidental where it is withheld.** This is not a system stumbling into consequential decisions by accident — every real behavioral connection found tonight (RiverBrain feeding council selection, the fitness gate, the council-rating blend) is the direct, traceable, dated result of someone identifying a specific gap and closing it on purpose. That's a genuinely well-governed pattern on the "yes" side of the ledger.

But the "no" side is not uniformly principled. Half of it is — Shadow and the attempt ledger are both real, considered non-decisions with a stated rationale, exactly as careful as the wired cases. The other half is not: one gap has simply never been visited by anyone in 71+ days, and the other is a live casualty of an unrelated, still-correct decision made before the thing it now silently damages was ever built. The practical lesson for this project's own stated discipline (report, then pause, then let a human decide) is that a review pass asking only "was this ever decided?" would correctly clear the collateral-damage case as "yes, decided" — the near-duplicate filter *was* deliberately built — while missing that the decision it clears was never actually about the thing currently suffering from it. Distinguishing "decided" from "decided about this specific case" is the check this project doesn't yet have, and the one this pass suggests would be worth having.
