# The Adversarial Learner Game

No gaming mechanism was built. No production code, evaluator, prompt, RiverBrain, AP-0, routing, or persistent state was touched. No prospective trial was run. Only this report was created.

**Grounded against**: `audits/2026-09-22_preregistered_persistent_routing_experiment_design.md`, `audits/2026-09-22_persistent_learning_experiment_premortem.md` (Codex), `audits/2026-09-23_trusted_boundary_reconciliation.md` (mine, same session), and fresh source reads performed across this session's own prior missions — not re-derived from scratch where already established, but never taken on faith either.

## 1. Executive verdict

**Rung 5 (bounded routing improvement, the mission's "Rung 1") is close to Counterfeit-Learner-robust once the trusted-boundary reconciliation's repairs are actually built and independently verified — a real, positive, non-trivial finding, not just more caution.** Working through the perfect-information attack (§10) against the fully-repaired design finds no cheaper reliable path to a positive verdict than the target phenomenon itself, *once* every named counterfeit channel is closed. **One new gap surfaces here that neither prior report named**: the structural-transfer (S-split) claim specifically remains counterfeitable, even after every Rung-5 repair, by a coarse structural-template recognizer that never touches the actual hidden convention — closing this requires one additional comparator (§9) not previously proposed. **Rung 7 (acquisition beyond the fixed strategy menu, the mission's "Rung 2") has no discriminating experiment at all right now** — every counterfeit path (composition, retrieval, latent-capability exposure) remains open, and closing it requires new methodology (a capability-availability sweep, a withheld rule family, a real strategy-composition mechanism) that is sketched here but not designed in full, per the mission's own instruction not to over-design future rungs. The two arrows — first unsupported and first counterfeitable — are **not the same arrow**, and conflating them would be a real mistake this report exists to prevent.

## 2. Formal game definition

**Player 1 — Learner (the Counterfeit Learner as a game-theoretic abstraction, not a psychological one).** Strategy space: which underlying mechanism to instantiate, from a genuine experience-dependent causal update to any of the counterfeit mechanisms enumerated in §5/§6. Objective: maximize the strength of the reported learning verdict per unit of genuinely acquired target capability — equivalently, minimize acquired capability subject to clearing the verdict threshold. This is a resource-minimization objective; information asymmetry (knowing the rules without knowing the protected draws) is the *lever*, not the objective itself.

**Player 2 — Evaluator/experiment designer.** Strategy space: evaluator architecture, task-generation process, information-disclosure policy, matched-cost/matched-information enforcement, the intervention battery (state substitution, restart, experience substitution, independent replication), and the statistical plan. Objective, in decision-theoretic terms: minimize the joint probability of a false-positive verdict (Type I: reporting the target claim when it is false) and a false-negative verdict (Type II: failing to credit the claim when it is true) — with an explicit, stated asymmetry toward avoiding Type I, since the cost of a wrongly-believed learning claim (per this project's own repeated findings about self-reported mechanisms that turned out hollow) is judged higher than the cost of a missed positive.

**Nature.** Draws the actual prospective/holdout task instances from a fixed, pre-committed generative process (AP-0's `worlds.py`/`tasks_v2.py`), controls task ordering if the protocol specifies randomization, controls hidden expected outputs until the trusted comparison step, and supplies irreducible backend sampling noise.

**Information.** The Learner, in the perfect-information framing (§10), knows the *entire design*: preregistration, claims ladder, success criteria, stopping rules, baselines, controls, evaluator architecture, strategy menu, persistence/restart/state-substitution protocols, transfer categories. It does **not** know the specific protected prospective/holdout task draws or their expected outputs, if and only if the trust boundary (§4 of the trusted-boundary report) is genuinely process-separated — this single fact is the entire load-bearing secret in the whole design; everything else can and should be public. The Evaluator observes only the Learner's actions, artifacts, and outcomes through its own instrumentation — it never directly observes whether the Learner's underlying mechanism is "genuinely causal" versus "table lookup dressed up," which is precisely the identification problem this whole exercise exists to solve.

**Hidden information.** Nature's protected draws/expected values (revealed only to the trusted comparison process, never to the Learner). The Learner's own true generative mechanism (hidden from the Evaluator, who infers it only through behavior under intervention).

**Timing and commitment.** Sequential, with the Evaluator moving first and *committing*: the full protocol (including feature-signature granularity, champion-selection procedure, task-order-randomization policy, and stopping rule) is hashed and frozen before Nature draws any prospective/holdout material, using the same `freeze.py`-style commitment mechanism already proven in this repository (`audits/2026-09-22_task_correctness_evaluator_qualification.md` §4, `audits/2026-09-22_preregistered_persistent_routing_experiment_design.md` §5). This commitment is the primary mechanism-design lever: it denies the Evaluator the ability to redefine success after seeing results, and denies the Learner the ability to reverse-engineer the specific holdout draws before they exist.

## 3. Operational definition of gaming

**Gaming = obtaining the experimental verdict for a given rung's claim while that specific rung's causal/capability claim is false.** This is deliberately narrow and rung-specific, not a vague "did this feel impressive" standard. A mechanism is **not** disqualified merely for being simple, table-based, deterministic, frozen-weight, externally scaffolded, or strategy-selection-only — per the mission's own instruction, and consistent with §9 of the operational bounded-learning definition already established in the trusted-boundary reconciliation. The disqualifying property is never the mechanism's sophistication; it is whether the specific, pre-stated causal chain for that rung (E→U→S0→S1→restart→D→O, matched cost, matched information, restart survival, and whatever else that rung's definition requires) actually holds.

## 5. Rung-1 (=Rung 5) counterfeit attack

Every named counterfeit channel, checked against what the trusted-boundary reconciliation already specifies as closed, versus what remains genuinely open if any repair is skipped:

- **Evaluator leakage**: closed by the §4 process-separated evaluator (trusted-boundary report) — real, confirmed fatal if skipped (`audits/2026-09-22_persistent_learning_experiment_premortem.md` §4, independently reproduced), closed if built.
- **Task-signature memorization**: closed by excluding internal metadata (task IDs, split labels) from the selector's feature signature — already specified.
- **Hidden state (RiverBrain, council, task classifier, circuit breaker)**: excluded by design (the standalone architecture never calls any of these); the one real, in-process exception — the circuit breaker — is resolved and controllable per the trusted-boundary report §13 (bypass via `stream_query_ollama()` directly, or one fresh process per decision).
- **Unequal compute / retry asymmetry**: closed by the matched final-inference-cost contract (§16 here, §14/§16 of the design report).
- **Task-order exploitation**: closed by pre-registered, randomized, twin-matched prospective-phase ordering (a real gap Codex found and this session's trusted-boundary report accepted, §10 there).
- **Classifier drift**: not applicable to the recommended minimal lookup-table selector (no classifier exists in it); would need its own tracking if the optional richer architecture is ever chosen.
- **Procedural-seed inference**: AP-0's fresh, dictionary-filtered pseudo-word convention design specifically resists exact-value reconstruction from a seed — **but a distinct, real, partial counterfeit survives this**: template/schema recognition via stable structural signatures (function-name patterns, argument shapes) that never requires inferring the hidden convention at all. This is Rung 6's problem, not Rung 5's — see §9.
- **Cache state (backend warmth)**: a fairness/timing concern, not a correctness-invalidating one, already classified separately (trusted-boundary report §12).
- **State-substitution incompleteness**: closed by the complete state-boundary enumeration (trusted-boundary report §12).
- **Feedback-channel leakage**: closed by the PASS/FAIL-only sanitized contract, sourced from the repaired evaluator specifically (§6 there).
- **Researcher selection / weak baseline**: closed by Codex's development/validation-split champion-selection procedure (trusted-boundary report §14 item 6).

**The critical question, answered plainly, per the mission's own instruction not to move the goalposts**: if the only surviving explanation, after every channel above is genuinely closed, is a persistent experience-updated routing table that causally, restart-survivingly, matched-cost-fairly improves hidden-task correctness beyond a competent frozen router and an identical non-updating twin — **that is not counterfeit. It is the target phenomenon.** Rung 5 was always defined at exactly this level of ambition; discovering that the mechanism satisfying it is "merely" a lookup table tells us *which* of the many possible implementations of bounded routing-learning occurred, which is itself a real, useful, non-trivial finding, not a consolation prize to be argued away.

## 6. Rung-2 (=Rung 7) counterfeit attack

Working through the mission's own list against FeralEcho's actual architecture (frozen 7B-class Ollama models with broad general-purpose pretraining, not a narrow specialist model): **retrieval of an existing latent procedure and exposure of model-native competence are both extremely plausible, not edge cases** — a general-purpose 7B-class coding model already solves a large fraction of small, textbook-shaped Python tasks zero-shot, so "the selector's chosen strategy finally elicited a correct answer" is far more likely to mean "the selector finally asked in a way the model already knew how to answer" (Rung-5-legitimate routing) than "the selector caused the acquisition of something new." Composition of existing strategies is a real, distinct possibility a pure re-weighting selector cannot itself do (it can only choose *among* strategies, not *combine* them) — but nothing in the current architecture tests for composition either, so this channel is simply unaddressed, not ruled out. Task-signature memorization and inference of a hidden rule from familiar templates both reduce to Rung 6's already-identified problem. Prompt/context changes, evaluator-structure exploitation, and lookup-table disguise are all Rung-5-shaped concerns, not genuinely new at Rung 7.

**The central, unresolved question — distinguishing "better selection among existing capabilities" from "acquisition of a genuinely new reusable procedure" — has no answer yet**, and stating that plainly is more honest than gesturing at a solution. Two things would be needed, neither built nor fully designed here: (1) a credible, pre-registered characterization of what the frozen model can already do *without* any FeralEcho-side experience-conditioning (§7); (2) a task domain containing a rule family genuinely never seen in *any* phase — development included, not just prospective-vs-holdout — so that success cannot be explained by any finite table over already-known conventions.

## 7. Capability-availability problem

A procedure's "availability" to a frozen foundation model is not binary; six real categories, in decreasing order of how directly checkable they are:

1. **Immediately elicitable** — works on the direct, unadorned prompt.
2. **Elicitable under a different prompt/strategy already in the fixed menu** — the selector's whole legitimate job, at Rung 5, is finding this.
3. **Latent but undiscovered by the menu** — would require a strategy *outside* the current fixed set, but the model could still produce it if asked correctly.
4. **Constructible from existing components** — some chaining/composition the current menu structure doesn't perform.
5. **Retrieved from model-native pretraining knowledge** — the skill exists in the weights, full stop, independent of any FeralEcho mechanism.
6. **Genuinely acquired from FeralEcho's own retained experience** — would not appear in the model's output distribution under *any* prompt without the causal contribution of the retained state.

**Provenance-aware framework, proposed, not built**: before any prospective phase, run a broad, pre-registered "capability sweep" against the frozen model *in isolation* (bypassing the selector entirely — direct prompt, few-shot with unrelated examples, chain-of-thought, role-prompted, and any other good-faith framing decided in advance) on the development-adjacent task distribution. Anything elicited by this sweep is provisionally categories 1-2; anything the sweep fails to elicit is provisionally categories 3-6 — **never proven absent, per the mission's own explicit instruction; only "not found by this specific, disclosed, good-faith search."**

**Evidence that could nevertheless support an acquired-capability claim, short of proof**: the sweep genuinely fails across the pre-registered framing set; the experience-conditioned selector's approach succeeds via a mechanism traceably different from anything the sweep tried (e.g., referencing intermediate structure no sweep framing surfaced); the success replicates across independent lineages, not a one-off sampling fluke; a state-substitution ablation removing the specific retained-state contribution makes the success disappear. This is a real, if imperfect, operational bar — reported honestly as a bar, not as proof.

## 8. Design the strongest Counterfeit Learner (on paper only)

Combining every plausible cheap mechanism against FeralEcho's actual architecture: a persistent signature table (legitimate at Rung 5, per §5) **plus** a coarse structural-template recognizer keyed on stable surface features that requires zero real convention-induction, even against genuinely novel pseudo-word conventions (per Codex's own §3 finding that template identity — aggregation/ranking/state-update shape — is recoverable independent of the hidden mapping) **plus** opportunistic compute-purchasing on tasks it already handles well, if matched-cost is not enforced **plus** any surviving evaluator leak, if §4's repair is incompletely applied **plus** simple reliance on the underlying model's own broad pretrained breadth for the majority of "improvement."

**How far does it get, and why does it stop there**: it reaches **Rung 5 reliably and legitimately** — nothing about this combination is disqualified there, per §5's own conclusion. It **plausibly reaches Rung 6** (S-split "structural transfer") specifically *if* the feature-signature granularity is coarse enough for the template-recognizer component to transfer its T-learned preference to S "for free," without any real induction over the hidden convention — this is the exact granularity danger the trusted-boundary reconciliation flagged (§8 there) but had not yet connected to a concrete adversarial construction. **It fails outright, honestly, at Rung 7** — it has no mechanism to produce a strategy outside its own fixed, closed menu; faking acquisition would require actually acquiring something, which is precisely what this combination cannot do by construction. This is the one rung where "the counterfeit fails" is not a design gap to close but a real, structural, load-bearing property worth preserving.

## 9. Game-theoretic equilibrium analysis

**Rung 5 is close to incentive-compatible once every channel in §5 is closed** — the genuine mechanism has no cheaper counterfeit alternative left standing that doesn't also satisfy the definition, which is exactly the condition mechanism design calls for.

**Rung 6 is not currently incentive-compatible, and this mission's analysis surfaces a concrete, new fix, not previously proposed in either prior report: a template-matched comparator.** Alongside Arms A (frozen champion), B (update-disabled twin), and C (adaptive selector), add a fourth, non-adaptive comparator — a signature table keyed on the *coarsest structural template category alone* (aggregation/ranking/state-update shape, input representation), explicitly **denied any access to the hidden convention itself** (it never sees, and never could learn, the pseudo-word mapping). If Arm C's S-split performance is statistically indistinguishable from this template-only comparator, that is decisive evidence the observed "transfer" is template recognition, not learning of the hidden convention. If Arm C beats even the template-only comparator on S, that is real, added evidence of something beyond surface-structure recognition. **This closes the exact gap §8's Counterfeit Learner exploits at Rung 6**, and should be added to the trusted-boundary report's own minimum-repairs list.

**On secrecy versus structural robustness, per the mission's own instruction**: the design should not lean on hiding *more* things — the perfect-information attack (§10) is specifically constructed to test whether the design survives without relying on the Learner's ignorance of its own workings. The only load-bearing secret in a well-designed version of this experiment is the specific protected task draws and expected outputs; everything else (the full method, the champion-selection procedure, the intervention battery) can and should be public, exactly as real pre-registered science already works.

## 10. Perfect-information attack

**A Learner that knows everything about the design except the actual protected draws cannot counterfeit Rung 5, provided the repairs hold.** With a genuinely process-separated evaluator, enforced matched-cost, a pre-committed champion/twin selection procedure, and randomized task order, knowing *how* the rules work grants no exploitable move: succeeding still requires the retained state to be actually causally responsible for a real correctness improvement on tasks whose content was genuinely unknown in advance. This is the sense in which a fully-repaired Rung-5 design is **identified** under perfect information.

**Rung 6, even under the fully-repaired Rung-5 design, is not perfect-information-robust — and this is a structural gap, not an information-asymmetry one.** Knowing the exact feature-signature granularity does not help a Learner exploit it; the template-recognition path benefits from the *structure of the design itself* (a coarse signature) regardless of what the Learner knows, which is precisely why §9's template-matched comparator, not more secrecy, is the correct fix.

**Rung 7 is decisively not perfect-information-robust, and more dramatically so than Rung 6**: a Learner that knows exactly what criteria will be interpreted as "acquisition" can specifically engineer its observable behavior (composing existing strategies, or presenting a retrieved latent capability) to satisfy those criteria, precisely because no criterion currently distinguishes composition/retrieval from genuine acquisition (§6). This is not a design flaw to be patched cheaply — it is the honest state of an unsolved measurement problem.

## 11. Goodhart analysis

| Metric | What happens if optimized directly |
|---|---|
| Correctness | Legitimate primary metric *if* the evaluator boundary and matched-cost hold; gameable via evaluator leakage or compute-purchase otherwise (§3/§4 of the trusted-boundary report) |
| Improvement slope (training curve) | Dangerous as a primary endpoint on its own — a rising curve says nothing about post-boundary held-out value (Codex's own statistical-attack table, premortem §11); descriptive only, never a claim by itself |
| Transfer score | Gameable by the coarse-signature template recognizer (§8/§9) unless the template-matched comparator is added |
| Persistence | Gameable by a state hash surviving a fake "restart" that is not a genuine fresh-process boundary — closed by the five-step restart/restoration chain (trusted-boundary report §13) |
| Novelty (fresh surface tokens) | Gameable by "novel surface, familiar structure" — fresh pseudo-words do not guarantee novel algorithmic content (Codex's §3 finding) |
| Bare state change | Nearly meaningless alone — any update produces *some* state change; the selected-action-under-matched-randomness test (§4 of the ladder table) is the real bar |
| Strategy diversity | Not itself evidence of anything — a policy can diversify superficially without any choice being better |
| Evaluator pass rate | The single most obviously gameable metric of all absent the §4 process-separation repair |
| Benchmark-generation difficulty | Gameable by conflating surface complexity (length, distractor tokens) with genuine reasoning difficulty (§12) |

**Recommendation, consistent with every prior report**: prefer causal-intervention-based endpoints (state substitution, restart/restoration, matched-twin/champion comparison, the new template-matched comparator) over any single aggregate score, always.

## 12. Benchmark-generation attack

A counterfeit "self-improving via self-generated benchmarks" system can generate problems that are superficially novel (different surface vocabulary, restructured phrasing) but structurally isomorphic to problems it already reliably solves; it can define its own "difficulty score" in a way that conflates surface complexity with genuine reasoning difficulty; it can "exceed" its own benchmarks trivially because it selected or tuned them to remain within its existing competence envelope; the entire loop is self-referential, with no outside anchor to notice the difference. This is the same structural pattern as every gaming mechanism found at lower rungs, recursively applied to the benchmark-*creation* step itself.

**Distinguishing evidence**: an independent, non-self-referential difficulty/competence oracle (a genuinely separate, more capable verifier, or a human expert, never involved in generating or solving the benchmarks) periodically re-certifies that the self-generated benchmarks are (a) genuinely outside the system's *prior* (pre-cycle) competence envelope, verified via the same capability-sweep methodology from §7, and (b) that solving them required more than surface-pattern completion, checkable by applying this same document's own intervention discipline (state substitution, matched cost, restart, independent replication) recursively to whichever mechanism does the generating and solving. **Absent this independent anchor, self-generated benchmark theater is fundamentally indistinguishable from genuine frontier expansion by construction** — the system positioned to notice the difference is the same system whose incentives created it.

## 14. Indistinguishability analysis

**Case A — remain distinguishable with the right intervention.** At Rung 5, once the full intervention battery (§5/§9/§10) is in place, genuine causal routing improvement and any counterfeit remain distinguishable in principle *before* the channels are closed, and become jointly satisfied (not merely indistinguishable, but actually the same fact) once they are closed — see Case B.

**Case B — become behaviorally indistinguishable because the surviving mechanism satisfies the operational definition.** This is Rung 5's actual resting state, stated plainly rather than hedged: once every named counterfeit channel is closed by design, whatever mechanism clears the bar **is** the target phenomenon, by the operational definition in §3 (gaming = achieving the verdict while the claim is false; if every way the claim could be false-while-the-verdict-holds is closed, then achieving the verdict entails the claim is true). Calling the survivor "gaming" at that point would be a direct contradiction of the definition being used, not a cautious hedge.

**Case C — observationally indistinguishable but causally different, and the claim is genuinely underdetermined.** This is Rung 6/7's actual resting state under the *current* design: the coarse-signature template recognizer and genuine narrow transfer produce identical observable holdout statistics, and nothing in the design as specified (even with every Rung-5 repair applied) yet separates them. **The scientific claim about structural transfer is underdetermined until the §9 template-matched comparator is built and independently verified**, and the claim about new-procedure acquisition is underdetermined until the §7 capability-sweep-plus-withheld-rule-family apparatus exists at all. This is a genuinely different epistemic situation from Rung 5's Case B, and the two must not be conflated in any future write-up.

## 15. Build the strongest discriminator (Rung 5, on paper only)

Combine, in one design: the process-separated evaluator (trusted-boundary report §4); a matched-cost, matched-information frozen champion and identical-architecture non-updating twin, both selected via Codex's pre-registered development/validation procedure; the complete E→U→S→D→O provenance chain with the circuit-breaker/fallback-path/seed audit resolved (trusted-boundary report §13); the five-step restart/restoration chain; Estimand-A-scoped experience substitution, explicitly not conflated with a full online rollout (§11 there); literal partition-disjointness verification; task-order randomization matched across twins; multi-lineage replication under a pilot-informed, pre-registered stopping rule. **Under this combined design, the Learner's cheapest reliable path to a positive verdict is to actually possess a real, causally load-bearing, retained state that improves decisions** — every cheaper alternative named across §5-§10 has been individually closed. This satisfies the mechanism-design objective for Rung 5 specifically. **It does not yet satisfy it for Rung 6** (needs the §9 addition) **or Rung 7** (needs methodology that does not yet exist, §6/§7/§8).

## 16. Matched-cost requirements

Reusing and confirming the fairness contract already specified across the design and trusted-boundary reports: model calls, tokens, retries, wall-clock, strategy evaluations, and evaluator calls are all metered per decision and balanced across arms at holdout evaluation time specifically (**matched final-inference cost**, not equal lifetime compute, per the correction already accepted in the trusted-boundary report §8/§14). Retrieval operations are not applicable here — retrieval is excluded from this design by construction. Arm C's prospective-phase acquisition cost is measured and reported separately, never silently absorbed into the correctness comparison. **Separating learning efficiency from test-time search expenditure**: the matched-cost holdout contract specifically forecloses "winning by spending more compute per decision" — a system that only appears to improve because it was allowed more retries or tokens at evaluation time is demonstrating a compute advantage, not learning, and this design's own construction (identical per-decision budget for every arm at holdout time) already excludes that confound once genuinely enforced, not merely stated.

## 17. Skeptical-review fixed point

**Round 1** (assume the report claims "FeralEcho demonstrated persistent experience-derived learning and novel transfer"): Codex's own §16 skeptical paragraph (`audits/2026-09-22_persistent_learning_experiment_premortem.md`), already adopted verbatim in the trusted-boundary reconciliation's §18 — same-module evaluator leak, ambiguous replay, uncontrolled history channels, the signature-table explanation left undisclosed. **Redesign to defeat it**: the full trusted-boundary repair (§4 there) plus Estimand A plus the complete state boundary plus the resolved circuit breaker — everything specified across the two prior 2026-09-22/23 reports.

**Round 2** (after those controls are added, the remaining skeptical explanation): *"Even granting a real process-separated evaluator, a real restart boundary, and a real matched-cost champion/twin comparison, the observed S-split 'transfer' could still be nothing more than a coarse structural-template recognizer that never touched the actual hidden convention at all — since the repaired design still contains no comparator that isolates template recognition from genuine convention-learning, and this project's own underlying model has broad enough pretrained competence that many 'novel' AP-0 tasks are solvable via surface-pattern completion regardless of any real induction."*

**Stop here, per condition B of the mission's own instruction**: this remaining explanation is not defeated by anything specified so far — the target (structural transfer specifically) **remains experimentally underdetermined** without §9's template-matched comparator. **This does not extend to Rung 5's own routing-improvement claim**, which Round 2's skeptic has no remaining purchase against — Rung 5 reaches Case B (§14: gaming and genuine become the same fact); Rung 6 reaches Case C (§14: genuinely underdetermined).
