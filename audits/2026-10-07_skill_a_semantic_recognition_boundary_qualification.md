The natural October 7 `.sort()` specimen contained the target defect. Its frozen one-site negation removal corrected all 24 directional failures and passed all 36 new mechanistic cases. Its rejection is a demonstrated recognition gap within this tested domain. It does not justify adding every `.sort()` occurrence to Skill A's authority.

## 2. What prior evidence actually authorizes

The [prospective authority reconstruction](skill_a_recognition_20261007/authority.md) preceded variant construction. Inspection covered the exact [production record](../memory/skill_ledger/skills/K2.T5.tag_priority_direction.v4.json), [acquisition candidates](../memory/experiments/skill_ledger/mvk/acquisition_candidates.json), [acquisition result](../memory/experiments/skill_ledger/mvk/acquisition_result.json), prospective [TRUE](../memory/experiments/skill_ledger/prospective/true_result.json) and [SUBSTITUTION](../memory/experiments/skill_ledger/prospective/substitution_result.json) records, and [BJQ raw outcomes](bjq1_20260930/O_results.json).

The supported relationship is precise. For ordinary entries `(name, integer score, tag)`, with higher score preferred, an independently specified tag order next, and smaller name last, an **ascending** sorting key selecting its **first** item needs a **positive** rank-map contribution when smaller numbers represent better tags. A negative rank contribution reverses tied-score tag preference. Removing that negation repairs this particular ordering mechanism when the remaining dataflow, return and ownership contracts are sound.

Historical causal evidence remains narrow: prospective TRUE 4/4 versus SUBSTITUTION 0/4, but three selected bodies were identical; BJQ G improved from 6/20 to 20/20 while H fell from 20/20 to 9/20. H's map encoded priority in the opposite numeric direction. This already refutes the claim that the local negation or a `sorted` call alone establishes repair authority.

The actual stored operational representation is:

```text
-tag_priority[x[2]] → tag_priority[x[2]]
node_type = UnaryOp
nearest enclosing call name = sorted
```

It contains concrete identifiers and index positions. It does not operationally encode canonical tag order, map orientation, ascending versus descending selection, which item is consumed, whether the edited site affects the result, or mutation ownership. The feature name and provenance prose are not a runtime semantic proof.

The enclosing-call requirement has a legitimate history: early application of the sorted-derived edit under `max()` failed. It was added to distinguish opposite aggregation directions. This mission tests where that useful restriction also loses equivalent cases; it does not dismiss it as arbitrary.

Prior evidence did **not** authorize arbitrary encodings, helper/dataflow forms, aliases, custom builtins, multiple sites, general repair portability, or safe autonomous application.

### Provenance discrepancy found and preserved

No pinned input had changed, and this discrepancy does not alter the frozen synthetic contrast. It weakens provenance integrity rather than showing a new learning failure.

## 8. What this says about representation

**Direct evidence:** p00 and n03 have byte-identical source but opposite independently specified priority contracts. The exact same edit repairs one and harms the other. Their hashes and contracts are preserved in the summary. A recognizer restricted to that source and Skill A's present fields cannot resolve this difference. Knowing the intended relation is essential.

**Direct evidence:** stored binding names and nearest-call identity cause verified equivalent programs to be rejected. Meanwhile map direction, selection direction and live edit-site relevance are not checked, allowing harmful/irrelevant matches. The current matcher is neither uniformly semantic nor uniformly conservative.

**Strong inference:** the dominant limitation is the **current learned representation plus its interface**, not merely one missing `sort` branch. The matcher implements the literal abstraction it was given. A broader identifier matcher alone would still lack the task contract and authority obligations. SA-R1's gains came from externally adding that missing knowledge and tightly restricting structure.

This does not prove that all VSL representations are incapable of semantics, that a general code analyzer is necessary, or that a new architecture would work. It demonstrates that **this skill record has not encoded the abstraction needed for the tested transfers**. More syntax exceptions are likely to move the boundary without answering what grounds safe authority.

## 10. Decision and smallest next experiment

**Primary: RECOGNITION-BRITTLE.** Unsafe matches coexist with brittle misses. The architectural diagnosis is representation-limited in the demonstrated sense above; neither blanket conservatism nor “just support `.sort()`” explains the findings.

Do not deploy SA-R1 from this result. To make such behavior production-capable would require a trustworthy source/binding for canonical task order, explicit encoding/selection/site/ownership checks, controlled `.sort()` dispatch and traceable use of the exact replacement. Existing consumer inputs do not supply those semantics. That is a separate design and independent qualification, not a justified one-line matcher patch.

The smallest useful next experiment is a **contract-and-binding representation qualification**, still research-only: determine whether one explicit task-relation/repair-obligation representation can recognize alpha-renamed and extracted equivalents while refusing opposite-contract and ownership counterexamples, without adding one rule per syntax form. Preregister a fresh small challenge set and keep acquisition versus investigator-authored information separate. A result that merely adds more syntactic special cases should close that approach, not trigger another patch ladder.

The discovered provenance mismatch also merits a separately reviewed correction/audit of index-to-hash attribution; this mission preserves it rather than changing historical evidence.

Only an independently qualified production change should face a **new independently selected naturalistic utility workload**. Do not reuse October 7 as the development benchmark. Defect-gated third-arm testing remains contingent on actual applications in that future comparison. Broad acquisition, a learning curve and claims of positive retained utility are not reopened by this diagnostic.

### Five answers

1. **Does Skill A recognize its learned defect across semantically irrelevant representation changes?** Inconsistently: 4/13 verified positives matched. Formatting and some surrounding changes survived; identifiers, extraction, tuple construction and `.sort()` often did not.
2. **Can recognition be broadened without authorizing harmful repairs?** Within the frozen restricted grammar, yes: one added `.sort()` positive and no negative authorizations, followed by a passed bounded challenge. This required new external semantic checks; general safe recognition and production readiness remain unestablished.
3. **Was the October 7 `.sort()` rejection appropriate conservatism or brittle recognition?** A demonstrated brittle refusal on this independently verified specimen. Retaining the production restriction remains justified until a safe replacement is separately qualified; indiscriminately admitting `.sort()` is unsafe.
4. **Is the immediate bottleneck the matcher or learned representation?** Both participate, but the deeper evidenced problem is the stored representation/interface. It preserves historical syntax without enough executable semantic authority. Source-identical opposite-contract cases cannot be solved by a source-only matcher patch.
5. **What is the smallest justified step toward measuring whether past learning makes future Echo better?** Qualify an explicit task-contract and repair-binding representation on fresh equivalents and harmful near-neighbors, then independently qualify any production change before another fresh net-utility workload. This mission supplies no new positive net-utility claim.
