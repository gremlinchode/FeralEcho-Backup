## 1. Executive summary

Below the seven-day audit's own inventory sits a second, earlier research arc (2026-09-16 through 2026-09-20) that the current Rung-1/accumulation-probe implementation track does not visibly build on, cite, or reject. That arc — the "zero-cost capability ceiling" investigation, its adversarial review, the resulting seven-experiment roadmap (E1–E7), a second adversarial review of that roadmap, a "persistent competence" experiment design distilled from it, two further adversarial/blind reviews of that design, and a fully frozen v1.0→v1.1 protocol — is the single largest body of methodologically rigorous, never-implemented research this project has produced. It was never run. Nothing in the current `app/experiments/rung1/` or `app/experiments/accumulation_probe/` code references it, and nothing in the reports themselves documents a decision to abandon it. This is the archaeology's central finding: **not a missing capability, but a missing decision record.**

A second, parallel and independently-evolved lineage — E5-mini (2026-09-16, nine reports, a genuine multi-round QUALIFIED→NOT-QUALIFIED→repair→NOT-QUALIFIED-again cycle) — appears, on file/module-name correspondence alone, to be the direct ancestor of the code that *did* get implemented (`app/experiments/accumulation_probe/`'s `oracle.py`/`sandbox.py`/`checker.py`/`builder.py`/`applicability.py` mirror E5-mini's own named components closely; no report or commit message states this explicitly). If that correspondence is real, it means one lineage (E5-mini) survived into production and the other (persistent-competence/candidate-tournament) did not, for reasons nowhere written down.

Three concrete, previously-underweighted pieces of evidence recovered in this pass materially bear on the Rung-1 roadmap's own assumptions:

1. **The persistent-competence adversarial review (2026-09-19) found the current five-mechanism candidate-tournament sweep was not exhaustive — it missed two real, live mechanisms** (`behavioral_state.py` and `self_model_claims.py`), one of which (`self_model_claims.py`) is arguably a *stronger* independently-evaluated candidate than RiverBrain, the mechanism every subsequent stage of this research program has centered on. Nothing since has re-run the candidate search with these included.
2. **A real, quantified demonstration that a naive experimental design can make a fixed capability gap indistinguishable from learning** — the frozen protocol v1.0's alphabetical tie-break bug, proven via synthetic simulation to reproduce a "0.877 vs 0.869 pass rate, apparently confirming learning" result from a completely fixed, non-learning policy. This is the single most concrete artifact in this project's entire research history of the "counterfeit learner" risk every subsequent mission (including this session's own Rung-1 work) has had to reason about from first principles, independently, with no citation back to where it was first proven.
3. **RiverBrain's headline discrimination result (77.8%) is a Simpson's-paradox artifact of one bad councillor, not a general correctness signal** (Tier-7, 2026-09-05) — and the report's own description of *how* the underlying score is computed is now stale relative to current source (Finding 91 changed the exact threshold Tier-7 describes, on the same calendar day).

No fabricated urgency is intended by leading with these three: this report also finds real, defensible reasons the current roadmap's narrower scope (Rung-1's single hidden-test gate, AP-0's oracle work) is a reasonable, cheaper first step than reviving the full frozen protocol wholesale. ## 6. Special Investigation 1 — Candidate tournament reconstruction

**Why the architecture won (RETRIEVED, `..._design.md`):** the design explicitly reused F2's real, already-verified sandbox pass/fail as the evaluator (cheapest, most trustworthy signal already in this codebase), a fresh carrier modeled on `self_model_claims.py`/`behavioral_state.py`'s deliberately simple, no-singleton, no-background-thread architecture (chosen specifically to avoid RiverBrain's own known writer-thread contamination risk, later confirmed real and unfixed by Tier-7 the same week), and a narrow consumer that *mirrors* `score_model()`'s selection logic in a fresh module rather than calling it directly — explicitly to avoid inheriting RiverBrain's own EMA-saturation and cross-contamination risks while still testing a structurally comparable claim.

**What was rejected and why (RETRIEVED):** the design's own ~10-mechanism L0–L3 classification table is explicit that RiverBrain itself was rejected as the *primary* experimental subject specifically because `pce-031` had already shown its score performs worse than chance against an independent criterion in real, uncommitted code — i.e., the design's authors already knew, before the adversarial review even ran, that the mechanism most people would reach for first was compromised, and chose a fresh-build approach instead of trying to rehabilitate RiverBrain in place.

**Whether assumptions hold against current source (OBSERVED, this mission):** the central premise — that `learn_from_rating()`/`learn_from_sandbox_outcome()` never write `model_task_stats`, while `learn()` and `learn_from_council_rating()` do — was independently re-verified this mission by direct grep against `echo_model_orchestrator.py`: exactly two `model_task_stats[model_name]` write sites exist today, both consistent with the design's own claim. The wiring gap the entire program is built around is confirmed still live, unfixed, as of this mission.

**Whether AP-0/Rung-1 recreated any part of this architecture (OBSERVED via grep, this mission):** no. `app/experiments/rung1/trusted_evaluator.py` and `candidate_worker.py`, and every file under `app/experiments/accumulation_probe/`, were grepped for `persistent_competence`, `frozen_protocol`, `candidate tournament`, and `candidate_tournament` — zero hits anywhere in the codebase outside the persistent-competence reports' own lineage (confirmed: only `2026-09-22_codex_ap0_independent_forensic_audit.md` and this session's own `2026-09-23_claude_seven_day_research_debt_audit.md` mention it at all, and — per that seven-day audit's own scope — only as a prior-week inventory item, not as a design input to AP-0/Rung-1's own architecture).

**Whether Rung-1 discarded it for a documented reason:** no such document was found. This is a genuine, load-bearing gap, not a negative finding dressed up as one: the mission's own instruction not to assume the current roadmap is correct applies exactly here. It is equally possible that (a) Rung-1's designers independently arrived at a narrower, cheaper first step and never felt the need to explain why they didn't first spend the very large budget the frozen v1.1 protocol requires (§11's own §5 partition table implies roughly 12,000 ledgered LLM generations — [EVALM-7] states this explicitly and flags feasibility as UNKNOWN even in the frozen text itself), or (b) the frozen protocol was simply never surfaced to whoever scoped Rung-1, a pure communication/continuity gap of the kind this project's own CLAUDE.md has documented dozens of times under a different name ("doc lags commit").

**Whether combining the tournament with Rung-1 tests something scientifically different:** yes, and this is a genuinely underexploited opportunity, not merely a completeness gripe. If Rung-1's hidden-test gate is validated first (cheaper, narrower), the frozen v1.1 Track C design is a directly reusable next step, not a redundant one: it already has a QUAL-4-grade null-effect/positive-control calibration built in, which Rung-1's own apparatus would still need to build from scratch if it ever attempted a "does retained context help" claim.

## 7. Special Investigation 2 — RiverBrain archaeology (the 77.8%/38.9% result)

**What was measured (OBSERVED/RETRIEVED, Tier-7):** a real, non-synthetic pre-Tier-4 snapshot of `memory/river_brain.pkl`'s `model_task_stats` (dated ~12 hours before the Tier-4 corpus's own generation), read via isolated `pickle.load()` (never via `get_river_brain()`, to avoid triggering the writer-thread contamination the same report goes on to disclose), was used to predict which of two candidate solutions — from real, historical Tier-4 disagreement events across three real models (`mlx:qwen3`, `qwen2.5-coder:7b`, `echo:latest`) — was correct, on 90 genuine single-pair, exactly-one-correct discriminating comparisons.

**Sample size and confounding model (OBSERVED, this mission, re-derived from the report's own table):** 90 total discriminating pairs; per-model accuracy on the underlying 84-candidate-each corpus (this is a *different*, larger denominator than the 90 pairs — the 84-per-model figures describe raw per-model accuracy across the whole corpus, not the paired comparison) shows `mlx:qwen3` 88.1% [79.5%, 93.4%], `qwen2.5-coder:7b` 83.3% [73.9%, 89.8%], `echo:latest` 53.6% [43.0%, 63.8%] — a Wilson 95% CI gap that does not overlap between `echo:latest` and either other model, but *does* overlap between the two stronger models. The confound is `echo:latest`: it is dramatically worse than the other two, and RiverBrain's real historical score correctly reflects this. The 77.8% aggregate figure is dominated by pairs involving `echo:latest`, where a low RiverBrain score for `echo:latest` correctly predicts it lost.

**Is this genuinely Simpson's paradox (OBSERVED, this mission, re-derived from the report's own numbers):** yes, in the precise technical sense — the aggregate discrimination rate (77.8%) reverses direction, i.e. drops *below chance*, on the one subgroup (the `qwen2.5-coder:7b` vs. `mlx:qwen3` pairing, 7/18 = 38.9%) that has the *largest* absolute score gap between the two candidates of the three possible pairings. A predictor that is more confident where it is more wrong, restricted to exactly the subgroup where its confidence should matter most, is the textbook shape of this confound, not merely "the effect is smaller in one slice."

**Does `score_model()` still consume this exact statistic today (OBSERVED, this mission):** yes — the two live write sites confirmed in §6 above feed the identical `model_task_stats[model][task_type]["mean"]` field `score_model()` reads, per Tier-7's own directly-traced data-flow reconstruction (RETRIEVED, independently structurally consistent with this mission's own direct source read).

**Does current RiverBrain state make this obsolete:** UNKNOWN. RiverBrain is a continuously-updating, unbounded-lifetime, `_MEAN_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW`-capped (200-observation) rolling statistic — the specific pre-Tier-4 snapshot Tier-7 analyzed is now roughly three weeks stale relative to this mission's own date, and per the window-cap mechanics, the live score today reflects only the most recent ~200 real observations per (model, task_type), not the snapshot analyzed. Whether `echo:latest`'s coding-task score has since improved, worsened, or stayed flat was not checked this mission (would require loading live RiverBrain state, which this mission's own read-only discipline and the known writer-thread risk both argue against doing casually).

**Does any experience-dependent signal predict future outcomes and survive restart:** RETRIEVED, not independently reproduced this mission — Tier-7's own scoped verdict is narrower than "RiverBrain is useless": its stated conclusion is that the score is "closer to a working detector for one specific bad councillor than a general-purpose correctness prior." This is a real, positive, if narrow, finding: a persistent, restart-surviving statistic (RiverBrain's pickle, confirmed independently by this project's own history to survive restarts) correctly flags one specific model's unreliability across a real, independent evaluation criterion it was never trained directly against (the corpus's own oracle-verified pass/fail, not RiverBrain's own training signal). That is a genuine, if modest, L2-or-better result by this project's own classification vocabulary, and it deserves to be stated plainly as a positive finding, not buried under the paradox headline.

**A live, previously-undisclosed drift, found this mission (OBSERVED):** Tier-7's own prose description of how the coding-task score is computed ("0 branches → score 2, 1-2 → score 3, 3+ → score 4") is now stale. Direct read of `echo_quality_scorer.py` lines 404–410 today shows `c < 6 → 3, else → 4` — the exact threshold CLAUDE.md's own Finding 91 (2026-09-05) changed, landing the same calendar day as the Tier-7 report itself (both commits — `f5d9b33` for Finding 91, `055b389` for Tier-7 — share the date 2026-09-05; which landed first within that day was not determined). This does not invalidate Tier-7's headline empirical result (which analyzed historical *scores*, i.e. already-computed `model_task_stats` means, not the scoring function's live behavior) — but it means Tier-7's own explanatory prose about *how* the score is computed can no longer be cited as an accurate description of current source without independent re-verification, which is exactly the "doc lags commit" pattern this project's own CLAUDE.md has documented dozens of times, now found inside a research artifact rather than a production doc.

## 8. Special Investigation 3 — Self-edit / quality-signal archaeology

**`_ast_complexity()` shortcut vulnerability (RETRIEVED from `pce-031`/CLAUDE.md Finding 91, cross-checked OBSERVED against current source this mission):** the underlying mechanism — a purely structural (AST branch-count) proxy for code quality, with the function's own code comment explicitly disclaiming any ability to catch "incorrect control-flow logic... without execution" (RETRIEVED, Tier-7's direct quotation) — is confirmed still live and unchanged in kind, only in threshold, by this mission's own direct read. The Finding 91 fix (2026-09-05) changed *where* the saturation ceiling sits (from `c>=3` to `c>=6`), based on the real observed distribution of 25 historical self-edit deploys — it did not, and does not claim to, close the underlying shortcut-vulnerability class itself (a candidate with genuinely wrong logic but real, non-trivial branching structure still scores the maximum 4).

**Its authoritative-over-deployment status:** RETRIEVED, not independently re-verified this mission beyond confirming the function's continued existence and current threshold — CLAUDE.md's own Finding 19 (self-edit's fitness gate rejects a candidate only if it scores *worse* than production) is the documented consumer, and this mission did not re-trace whether that consumption is still live exactly as described.

**The (4,4) fitness-score tie claim across all seven deployed candidates:** RETRIEVED from both the python-capability-gap audit and the zero-cost-ceiling report, cross-confirmed between two independently-authored sources (the capability-gap audit's own Codex self-audit independently recomputed "all seven deployed records report fitness_score=4, production_score=4" directly from `memory/self_edit_attempt_ledger.jsonl` and found it CONFIRMED against the earlier report's identical claim) — this is the strongest kind of cross-document corroboration available in this archaeology (two independently-authored recomputations from the same raw ledger, both landing on the identical figure), though neither recomputation was re-run a third time by this mission itself, so it remains RETRIEVED rather than REPRODUCED here.

**Has this contaminated any historical "learning"/"improvement" evidence:** yes, directly, and this is one of the cleanest causal chains recovered in this archaeology. Before Finding 91's threshold fix, all 25 real historically-retained self-edit backups scored the identical maximum (4/4) under the pre-fix threshold (`c>=3`), meaning the fitness gate that is supposed to reject non-improvements *could not discriminate a genuine improvement from a lateral or even slightly worse move* for the entire history it has been operating over — every one of the seven real deployed self-edits that "passed" the fitness gate did so via a tie, not a demonstrated improvement, for the whole period this scoring function has existed. Finding 91's fix is dated 2026-09-05 — meaning any of the seven deployed self-edits predating that date "passed" a gate that was, at the time, structurally unable to fail a tie in either direction on the vast majority of real candidates (19/25, 76%, of the real historical distribution). This is a load-bearing negative finding for any claim that self-edit's own deployment history demonstrates genuine, evaluator-confirmed improvement over time.

**(10) DESIGN ONLY — the strongest falsifiable persistent-learning experiment buildable purely by recombining already-existing FeralEcho mechanisms, with no new architecture:**

Combine three already-existing, already-built pieces with zero new code: (a) `river.bandit`'s `EpsilonGreedy` or `ThompsonSampling` policy (already installed, zero marginal cost, §9/§13), fed by (b) the real, already-flowing, currently-disconnected sandbox-outcome signal (`learn_from_sandbox_outcome()`'s real pass/fail data, currently computed and discarded rather than routed anywhere useful), consumed by (c) a read-only, offline replay harness scoped exactly like the already-existing `river.bandit` "smallest experiment" sketch in the python-capability-gap audit (§7 of that report, RETRIEVED): replay RiverBrain's real historical `(model, task_type, sandbox_outcome)` observation log — which already exists on disk, unused for this purpose — through a fresh, in-memory-only `river.bandit` policy object, and compare, on held-out slices of that same real historical log, whether the bandit's would-have-chosen worker differs measurably and favorably from what the current flat-mean `score_model()` selector actually chose at the time. This requires: zero new dependency (river.bandit ships today); zero production code change (a pure offline replay script, isolated exactly as `install_isolation()`/Tier-8's redirect-before-first-access pattern already establishes); and a falsifiable, pre-specifiable claim (the bandit policy's counterfactual pass rate on held-out real historical episodes exceeds the flat-mean selector's actual historical pass rate by a pre-registered margin, tested with the same one-sided paired-comparison discipline the frozen protocol's own [STAT] machinery already specifies). It is falsifiable because a null or negative result (the bandit would have chosen no better, or worse, than the existing selector) is a clean, reportable, real finding either way — and, per the Codex self-audit's own explicit caution (§2, item C3.1 corrections table: "arbitrary historical log replay can tell us which unselected model would have performed better — not without counterfactual outcomes or defensible logged-policy support/propensities"), the design must be honest that this offline-replay approach can only bound, not conclusively prove, counterfactual superiority, since the historical log was generated under a different (non-bandit) selection policy and therefore under-samples whatever the bandit would have chosen to explore. This limitation should be stated in the experiment's own report, not discovered by a future adversarial review.

**(12) After this investigation, what is the strongest learning claim FeralEcho's existing evidence actually supports, and where exactly does the evidence stop?**
The strongest supportable claim is: **FeralEcho has at least one real, restart-surviving, behaviorally-consequential, experience-dependent adaptation mechanism (RiverBrain's coding-task ranking) whose demonstrated value is narrow and specific — reliably detecting one particular underperforming model — rather than general-purpose quality discrimination, and at least one other real mechanism (`behavioral_state.py`) that achieves a comparably rigorous state-to-behavior chain while explicitly and honestly declining to call itself learning at all.** The evidence stops firmly before any claim of: general capability improvement over time (no experiment in either lineage read this mission has ever completed a real, non-mocked, non-synthetic run at meaningful scale); accumulated competence across repeated, unrelated learning episodes (the one design built specifically to test this, the frozen v1.1 protocol's §12, has never been executed); or genuine transfer to structurally novel, held-out tasks (E5-mini's own real-model run never happened; its mocked apparatus never cleared qualification). Everything beyond the narrow, scoped claim above is either a well-specified but unexecuted design, a partially-connected mechanism whose consequence is currently self-referential or bookkeeping rather than independently verified, or an explicitly-disclaimed non-learning scaffold performing its stated role honestly. This is a conservative conclusion, consistent with this mission's own instruction to prefer a modest, well-evidenced claim over a stronger, momentum-driven one — and consistent with the honest character of nearly every individual report read this mission, each of which, read carefully, already says almost exactly this about itself.
