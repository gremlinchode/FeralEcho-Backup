Date: 2026-09-16. Reviewer: Claude M5, independent adversarial review (fork). Target: `audits/2026-09-16_feralecho_zero_cost_capability_ceiling.md`. Read-only. No implementation.

## 1. Executive verdict

Codex's report is unusually careful for a self-authored capability assessment — its own evidence-tier discipline is real (every spot-checked OBSERVED claim in §3 below verified exactly), and it already refuses several tempting overclaims (no "percentage of maximum capability," explicit "ties are not improvement," explicit flagging of shared model ancestry undermining evaluator independence). That is the correct baseline to evaluate against, and it makes this review's job harder than knocking down a sloppy report — the failures found here are mostly *structural gaps in the experimental program*, not factual errors in the diagnosis.

**The central finding of this review: even if E1 through E7 all succeed exactly as specified, the resulting evidence does not establish sustained, independently measured improvement in future competence.** It establishes, at best, a single instance of Level 4 (Transfer) on the claims ladder in §17 — one measured pre/post comparison. Nothing in the proposed program is longitudinal. Codex's own Decision Rule (§20 of the source report) names "a reproducible, versioned longitudinal trial showing increasing held-out task success at fixed compute" as the thing that would strengthen the accumulated-competence claim — and then does not propose one. This is not a minor omission; it is the gap between what the roadmap can prove and what its own executive summary implies it is building toward.

## 4. Learning-definition attack

Codex's definition ("an update counts as learning only when prior evidence changes a future decision measurably; a useful learner additionally improves an independently meaningful outcome") is directionally correct but under-specifies the boundary between *decision-changing* and *outcome-improving* — specifically, it never states what distinguishes genuine procedure reuse from a system that has simply gotten better at **retrieving and re-emitting a prior correct answer**, which is a retrieval property, not a competence property.

**Q1 — weakest evidence that should legitimately be called "learning":** the scheduler's prompt-history downweighting (`emergent_scheduler.py`, per Codex's own §4 table row "Scheduler history") — repeated/high-scored prompts get deprioritized, weak areas get boosted. This is real, cheap, behaviorally verified adaptation from experience. It is also nearly trivial: a frequency cache would produce the same external signature. Worth calling "learning" only in the loosest behaviorist sense — closer to habituation than competence.

**Q2 — stronger evidence required for "accumulated competence":** a frozen evaluator, a held-out task family, and **repeated measurement across at least three independent time points** (not two) showing monotonic or at least non-decreasing improvement, with the same family re-tested after intervening unrelated activity to rule out a one-time treatment effect. None of E1–E7 as specified do this — see §11 below.

**Q3 — could E1/E5 succeed while FeralEcho merely gets better at retrieving/reproducing prior solutions?** Yes, directly. E5's own "POSSIBLE CONFOUNDS" list names "hidden semantic duplicates" but the **INTERVENTION** and **SAMPLE SIZE / TRIAL PLAN** sections never specify a concrete duplicate-detection mechanism (e.g., an embedding-distance floor between each withheld variant and every teaching task, checked and reported before unblinding). Absent that floor, "withheld variant" is defined by the task designer's intent, not by a measured property of the data — exactly the gap this project's own Tier-3→Tier-4 research arc (2026-09-03/04, cited in this same repo) already demonstrated can silently inflate an effect that later fails to replicate.

**Q4 — evidence that would demonstrate transfer rather than memorization:** success on variants with renamed symbols, reordered inputs, and paraphrased problem statements that share no surface n-grams with the teaching examples; success when the *same stored procedure* is applied by a **different generator model** than the one whose attempts produced it (cross-model transfer — genuinely absent from E5's design, which never varies the generator between teaching and testing); and a **negative control** — deliberately near-miss tasks the procedure should *not* apply to, where correct behavior is recognizing inapplicability rather than forcing a match. E5's 24 out-of-scope cases are a version of this, but they test *harm avoidance*, not *correct non-application recognition* (a system that always ignores procedures entirely would also pass the harm-avoidance bar).

**Q5 — evidence of sustained accumulation vs. a one-time treatment effect:** exactly the longitudinal gap named in §1. A single before/after measurement, however well-controlled, cannot distinguish "the system learned something durable" from "this particular procedure happened to transfer once." Distinguishing them requires the same held-out family re-measured at multiple points with new, *unrelated* procedures added between measurements, to test for both retention and interference.

## 5. Outcome/attempt-contract attack

Codex's Tier-0 contract (task ID, attempt ID, model identity, prompt, context manifest, evaluator version, etc.) is a reasonable checklist, but three concrete gaps matter for whether it is actually buildable as specified in this codebase:

**Retry independence is not resolved, only named.** This codebase's own self-edit retry mechanism (per CLAUDE.md's Finding 22 Batch 4, "retry-then-succeed self-edit cycles") conditions a retry's prompt on the prior attempt's real error message. A retry is therefore not a fresh, independent draw — it is a second attempt with privileged information a genuinely new attempt would lack. Codex's contract records "parent attempt/retry" as a field but the E1 sample-size plan ("40 distinct tasks, three archived/isolated candidates each") never states whether retries are excluded from the independence assumption the statistical test requires. If any of the three candidates per task is a retry of another, the effective N for the false-promotion calculation is smaller than 120, and the true value is currently unknowable from the report as written.

**Double-credit already has a real, unused mechanism to build on.** Codex's own §4 states "reward should attach to action attempt and version, not merely the name of a model" — correct, but this project already has a partial answer sitting unused: `synthesis_integrity_log.jsonl` (built 2026-09-02, per this repo's own commit history) tracks per-candidate SHA1 hashes and whether synthesis preserved agreed-upon definitions. None of E1–E5's designs reference reusing or extending this mechanism for attribution — a concrete, cheap, already-half-built piece of infrastructure the roadmap doesn't credit or route through.

**Model digest freezing is asserted as a requirement without a confirmed mechanism.** Codex's own §8 states plainly that "a model tag do[es] not prove loaded Python bytes or a model digest for a historical call" — this is the right level of caution, but the outcome contract's own requirement list (§5 of the mission brief: "actual model digest, quantization") is never confirmed as obtainable from Ollama's real API in this environment. If it isn't cheaply obtainable, Tier 0 as specified cannot actually be built to the standard Codex itself sets for it — this is a real, unresolved feasibility question, not just a nice-to-have field.

## 6. Evaluator-independence attack

This is where the source report's own evidence undermines its own experimental design most directly. §9 states, as an OBSERVED finding: **"Echo and llama3:instruct are closely related; deepseek-r1:7b and qwen2.5-coder:7b inventory report qwen2 ancestry."** This is Codex's own verified fact, not this review's inference.

Apply it to the proposed experiments:
- **E3** compares council strategies using models from a 9-model pool where at least two pairs share direct lineage. If a generator and part of an "independent candidates + deterministic selection" scoring path draw from related-ancestry models, agreement between them is not independent confirmation — it is partially correlated error, exactly the failure mode Codex names generically in §6 ("an LLM evaluator shares model ancestry or prompts with the generator") but does not check against its own §9 finding when designing E3's actual arms.
- **E7** (optional peer review via Claude/Codex) is the only experiment in the program with a *genuinely* architecturally independent evaluator (a different company, different training pipeline, different weights entirely) — and it is also the lowest-priority, most speculative experiment in the roadmap (Tier 2, "optional," explicitly not load-bearing). The single strongest evaluator-independence guarantee available to this project is being used for the least consequential experiment.
- For objective coding tasks, hidden tests (E1) sidestep the ancestry problem correctly — a test suite has no model ancestry. This is the right design choice for E1 specifically, and should be treated as the template: **any experiment whose evaluation cannot be reduced to a deterministic test should be treated as evaluator-contaminated by default in this specific model pool**, not just "correlated error is a possible confound."

FeralEcho needs the five separated evaluation types the mission brief asks about: safety (already real — F1/F2/F3, deterministic), functional correctness (real for code via sandbox execution, absent for anything else), regression (partially real via the fitness-tie gate, which Codex correctly shows is too weak — accepts ties), transfer (does not exist as a category anywhere in current code), and authority (real — `EDIT_FORBIDDEN_TARGETS`, human-confirmation gates). Only two of five are load-bearing today; the roadmap's Tier 0 addresses evaluation validity for the first two well but does not propose a distinct transfer-evaluation mechanism, leaving E5 — the highest-priority experiment — without a dedicated, non-model-judged evaluator design for its non-code task families (E5's 12 task families are not stated to be code-only).

## 7. E1 adversarial review

**Attack: the candidate pool is likely drawn from a population already selected by the mechanism being tested.** E1's own text says "three archived/isolated candidates each" for 40 tasks — the word "archived" strongly implies reuse of historical self-edit attempts, i.e., candidates generated and screened under the existing heuristic tie-accepting gate this experiment exists to replace. If the 40-task/120-candidate pool is drawn from that history, its candidate diversity is already shaped by whatever the old gate systematically over- or under-produced (e.g., if the old gate rarely rejects, the archive is dominated by near-ties, which is exactly the hardest case for the new gate to discriminate — a plausible mechanism for **either** an inflated or deflated apparent improvement, undetectable from the report as written). This confound is not on E1's own "POSSIBLE CONFOUNDS" list and should be.

**Attack: ambiguous statistical unit.** 40 tasks × 3 candidates = 120 evaluable units. The success criterion ("≥10-point lower false promotion... without over five-point loss in true-improvement recall") never states whether the denominator is 40 (task-level, correct) or 120 (candidate-level, pseudo-replicated — three candidates from the same task are not independent draws of "was this task correctly gated," since the underlying task difficulty is shared). This needs to be pinned to task-level before any confirmatory run, not left implicit.

**Attack: reject-everything can look like success under the stated criterion.** A gate that rejects all 120 candidates trivially achieves zero false promotions. The failure criterion correctly names this ("apparent success achieved by rejecting everything") but the *success* criterion doesn't cross-reference it numerically — "over five-point loss in true-improvement recall" is the only guard, and recall against what denominator (true improvements identified vs. true improvements that existed in the pool) is not specified given that "true improvement" itself requires the same contaminated-pool problem above to be resolved first.

**If E1 succeeds exactly as specified, what is licensed?** That the hidden-test gate discriminates better than the heuristic tie-accepting gate *on this specific 40-task archived pool, evaluated by these specific hidden tests, for this specific generator*. **What is not licensed:** that the gate generalizes to tasks outside this pool's shape, that it discriminates on non-code tasks, that promoted candidates represent genuine capability growth rather than passing a harder filter (a candidate can pass a better filter by luck or by narrow overfitting to the specific hidden test, same as any test-set), or anything about durability over time.

## 8. E2 adversarial review

E2's controls (frozen corpus, equal token budgets, held-out entire source episodes) are reasonably strong and correctly separate "recall@k improves" from "answer correctness improves" as distinct metrics — this is one of the better-controlled experiments in the program.

**Remaining attack: question construction from the corpus itself.** If the 60 questions are authored by looking at what's in the corpus (a natural way to build 60 questions across "exact-symbol, episodic, temporal-conflict, no-answer" strata), the questions are non-independent of the retrieval system's own indexing choices — a corpus-derived question is more likely to be phrased in terms the embedding model already handles well. E2's design doesn't state whether questions were authored blind to the corpus's actual retrieval behavior. This is a real, fixable gap (freeze questions before ever running retrieval against them, not just before scoring answers) but is currently unaddressed.

**E2 measures better memory retrieval, not better FeralEcho competence, as designed.** Even a clean E2 success only shows the retrieval layer surfaces more relevant text at equal token cost — it says nothing about the downstream reasoning that consumes that text, and should not be cited as evidence toward the broader capability-growth claim without that caveat attached every time it's referenced later.

## 9. E3 adversarial review

This gets the most scrutiny per the mission brief, because this exact codebase had a real, independently-confirmed condition-integrity failure in a directly analogous experiment 24 hours before this report was written (`audits/2026-09-15_codex_task_type_independent_review.md`: "Condition A was not direct: it ran the same council treatment as B" — an invalid control that the original experimenter's own adversarial-falsification pass missed and an independent review caught).

**E3's design does not yet defend against the exact same failure mode.** The BASELINE arm is described as "actual current council route, verified by recorded requests" — good, this explicitly requires verification, learning from the September 15 incident. But the **INTERVENTION** arms ("direct best-installed-model; tool-first; independent candidates plus deterministic selection") carry no equivalent verification requirement in the text. "Direct best-installed-model" is precisely the condition that failed silently on 2026-09-14/15 — a `disable_direct_echo_bypass_for_personal()`-style call that was set once and never restored, or a task-type flag that didn't actually route where its name implied. E3 as written would need to independently prove, per arm, that the intervention actually executed the code path its name claims — the same three-part proof (intercepted call counts, raw output content inspection, and independent re-derivation from source, all three used in the September 15 review) — not just for the baseline.

**Required condition-integrity manifest** (per the mission brief's own request), concretely, for every arm of E3: which models were actually called and how many times (verified against real logged call records, not the experiment script's own self-report); the actual system/user prompts sent, byte-for-byte, not the intended template; whether TOOL-LIST was present (this codebase's own September 14/15 research found TOOL-LIST eligibility and delivery are two separately-gated things — eligibility alone does not prove delivery); whether the synthesis/agreement-bypass code path fired or was skipped; actual token/context budgets used; and model residency/loading order, since this project's own §9 (Codex's report) already flags that sequential loading is a real, measured cost confound. Without this manifest **produced and checked before any outcome is interpreted**, E3 risks reproducing the exact bug this codebase already paid to discover once.

**Equal-compute vs. natural-latency are correctly flagged as needing separate analysis** — this is right, and worth reinforcing: a strategy that wins on natural latency but loses under matched compute is answering "is council worth its real cost" (an operations question), while the matched-compute comparison answers "is council's architecture better, holding resources fixed" (a design question). Conflating them, as many single-headline capability claims tend to, would misrepresent whichever one wasn't actually tested.

## 10. E4 adversarial review

**Attack: ≥90% interrupted-job recovery is achievable by pure workflow replay, and E4 does not clearly rule this out.** The mission brief's own framing is exact: an executor that persists a fixed, pre-determined action sequence and resumes it after a crash can hit high recovery rates while never adapting to anything. E4's 24 tasks are described only as "multi-step" — nothing in the design requires that at least some tasks contain a **genuine environment change mid-execution** (a file that moved, a dependency that changed, an assumption that became false) that forces real replanning rather than simple state-machine resumption. Without at least one such forced-replanning stratum, E4 cannot distinguish autonomy from checkpointing, and its own success criterion would be satisfied by either.

**Attack: "zero unauthorized writes" is doing a lot of quiet work.** The failure criterion correctly includes "any authority violation," but the sample size (24 tasks, two executions per arm = 48 runs) is small for confidently detecting a *rare* authority violation — a violation rate of even 5% would have a real chance of never appearing in 48 trials. This should be stated as a stated limitation, not left implicit; a clean E4 run does not license "the executor is safe," only "no violation was observed in 48 trials at this task distribution."

**Human authority vs. autonomy — correctly separated in principle, worth restating concretely:** an executor that asks for human approval *less often* is not thereby more autonomous by this review's own claims-ladder (Level 6 requires the human not to be choosing the task family or writing success criteria — asking fewer permission questions on a fixed task set is orthogonal to that). E4's design does not conflate these, which is a real strength worth crediting explicitly.

## 11. E5 adversarial review — highest priority

Working through the mission brief's specific attack list against what E5 actually proposes:

- **Semantic duplication — NOT addressed.** No embedding-distance floor or structural-transformation requirement is specified between teaching tasks and withheld variants. This is the single most exploitable gap in the entire program: a "withheld variant" chosen by a human author who already knows the teaching task's solution can trivially be a near-paraphrase without any measurement catching it.
- **Procedure leakage — NOT addressed.** The design does not specify whether the entity authoring/verifying each family's procedure has visibility into the 5 withheld variants during authoring. If it does (even implicitly, by whoever selects "12 task families" already having the full 8-task set in mind), the procedure can encode family-specific answers rather than general method, and "freeze procedures before testing" (E5's stated control) does not prevent this — freezing prevents the procedure from being *edited* after seeing results, but not from being *written with foreknowledge* of the full task set.
- **Model prior — adequately addressed.** The no-memory control arm directly tests this; credited as a real strength.
- **Retrieval advantage — adequately addressed.** The equal-token episodic-memory arm directly isolates "more/better tokens" from "structured procedure specifically," which is the correct control; credited.
- **Family contamination — NOT addressed.** No retrieval-namespace isolation between families is specified. Given this project's own real, documented FAISS index (128,837 entries per Codex's §6, a single shared vector space across all content types), a family-B query could surface family-A's procedure text via ordinary semantic proximity unless family-scoped filtering is explicitly required during the transfer-testing phase.
- **Evaluator leakage — same as §6 above; not specifically re-addressed for E5's own task families**, which are not stated to be code-only (E1/E6 are code-heavy; E5's families are open-ended), meaning E5 likely needs model-judged evaluation for at least some families and inherits the full §6 ancestry problem without a stated mitigation.
- **Selection bias / survivorship — NOT addressed.** Nothing specifies what happens to a family whose teaching-task attempts fail. If failed-teaching families are silently dropped rather than retained as a **zero-procedure control condition**, the reported transfer rate is measured only over families where teaching happened to succeed — survivorship bias inflating the headline number.
- **Negative transfer — adequately addressed** via the 24 out-of-scope cases and the ≤5-point regression bound; credited, though see Q4 above (harm-avoidance is not the same as correct non-application recognition — a system that ignores procedures entirely also clears this bar).
- **Version compatibility, temporal persistence, accumulation — explicitly NOT addressed by design, and this is the most important gap in the entire report.** E5 is a single before/after measurement. It cannot, by construction, show whether a procedure survives a restart weeks later, whether it survives a model swap, or whether learning procedures 1–10 causes procedure 11 to interfere with procedure 3's held-out performance. Codex's own report names all three as open Unknowns (§20, items implied by the Decision Rule) and then does not design an experiment for any of them.

**What would convince this review that FeralEcho demonstrated genuine reusable competence rather than sophisticated retrieval:** success that survives (a) renamed/restructured surface form, (b) a different generator model than the one that authored the procedure, (c) a measured embedding-distance floor from every teaching example, and (d) re-measurement after at least one intervening, unrelated procedure has been added to memory — all four simultaneously, not any one alone.

**What would demonstrate cumulative competence growth over time (the genuinely missing E8):** a longitudinal design — pick 3–5 of E5's task families, measure held-out performance at time T0 (before any procedures exist), teach and test procedures for unrelated families over several real intervening days/weeks of normal FeralEcho operation, then **re-measure the original families at T1, T2, T3** without re-teaching them. Rising or stable performance across T0→T3 with no re-teaching is the actual signature of accumulation; a single T0→T1 delta, however clean, is not.

## 12. E6/E7 boundary review

E6 (model/backend swap) is well-designed for what it claims — matched budgets, warm/cold separated, a memory preflight before any 27B admission — and correctly treats "is orchestration or the base model the constraint" as an open empirical question rather than assuming orchestration wins, which is the right posture given this project's own Tier-4 research (2026-09-03/04) already found synthesis measurably *loses* raw candidate information relative to what the underlying models could produce. E6 should arguably run **earlier** in the roadmap than Tier 5 currently places it — see §14 below.

E7 (optional peer review) is honestly scoped ("no dependence on peers for local tasks... zero new monetary charges") and its own resilience-replay design (peer unavailable in half the trials) is a genuine strength most of the other experiments lack — it directly tests the graceful-degradation property the architecture section claims ("core execution degrades gracefully if no collaborator is present") rather than just asserting it.

## 13. Statistical independence and power review

No proposed experiment states its statistical unit with full precision (task vs. candidate vs. attempt), which matters differently per experiment:

- **E1**: 40 tasks / 120 candidates — unit should be task; as written, ambiguous (§7).
- **E2**: 60 questions, two seeds each — 120 trials; questions are plausibly the correct unit, seeds are not independent questions, and this is handled correctly by not treating seed-repeats as new N.
- **E3**: 48 tasks, two seeds per strategy — same shape as E2, reasonably handled.
- **E4**: 24 tasks × two executions = 48 — small for rare-event (authority-violation) detection, as noted in §10.
- **E5**: 12 families × 5 withheld = 60 transfer tasks per arm, but the correct clustering unit is **family**, not individual transfer task — five withheld tasks from the same family are not independent draws of "did transfer work," since they share whatever the procedure got right or wrong about that family's structure. Family-clustered uncertainty is mentioned in the success criterion text but not carried through the sample-size justification, which still describes "60 transfer tasks" as if that were closer to the effective N than "12 families" is.

None of the proposed sample sizes should be read as confirmatory-power-justified as currently stated — Codex's own text is honest about this ("Sample sizes are concrete pilot/confirmation plans, not claims of adequate power for every effect") and this review agrees with that self-assessment; the correction here is narrower: **the family-vs-task unit ambiguity in E5 specifically should be resolved before any pilot is run**, since it changes the effective sample size by 5×, which changes whether the proposed pilot can even estimate variance sensibly.

## 14. Cross-experiment contamination

Real, concrete contamination vectors, given this is a persistent, always-running system, not a laboratory:

- **E1 candidates entering memory and contaminating E5** — plausible and unaddressed. If E1's 120 evaluated candidates get logged to `interaction_log.jsonl` (this project's own default behavior for essentially everything, per Codex's §5 table: 21,382 rows, only ~366 tagged as anything experiment-specific), E5's later procedure-authoring step could retrieve E1 candidate text via ordinary episodic memory, silently pre-seeding the "no-memory" or "episodic-memory" control arms with E1-derived content.
- **RiverBrain updates during E3 altering later arms** — real risk this project has already been burned by once (Tier-8's RiverBrain isolation gap, `audits/tier8_experimental_isolation_forensic.md`, found the background writer thread persists regardless of proxy patching, requiring a `RIVER_BRAIN_PATH`-redirect-before-first-access fix). Any of E1/E3/E5/E6 that touches `get_river_brain()` inherits this exact risk unless the same redirect pattern is applied — this is a **known, previously-fixed-once class of bug in this exact codebase**, not a speculative concern.
- **Autonomous workers creating memory during experiments** — this project's own `conversation_activity.is_conversation_active()` gate (built specifically to make autonomous loops defer to real conversations) has no equivalent "defer to a running experiment" signal. An experiment that doesn't explicitly throttle `autonomous_loop`/`emergent_scheduler`/`echo_projects_autonomy` risks a background worker writing to the exact memory store an experiment is trying to hold fixed, mid-run.

**Isolation recommendation:** the Tier-8 pattern (redirect the relevant state path before first access, in-process) is proven and cheap for RiverBrain specifically; it does not generalize automatically to the vector index, SQLite/task store, or self-edit cooldown state, each of which would need its own redirect verified the same way (empirically, against the real production file, confirming zero writes landed there — not assumed from reading the patching code). A frozen repository snapshot plus disabled autonomous workers for the experiment's duration is the more defensible default until each individual state path has been through the same isolation-verification exercise RiverBrain already has.

## 15. Architecture attack

Answering the 15 numbered questions concisely, prioritizing the ones with concrete evidence available in this codebase's own history:

1. **Centralizing state as a brittle single point of failure — real, and understated.** This codebase's own Findings history (CLAUDE.md's 90+ numbered findings) shows essentially every new stateful mechanism ever added here has shipped with at least one real concurrency bug found only after the fact (the log-retention double-rotation, the salience-state race, four separately-fixed shared-lock gaps in one single evening per Finding 41 B1/B2/B4/B5). A centralized task-kernel transactional store is a larger, more central piece of new stateful infrastructure than any single one of those — the base rate for "ships with a real race condition" in this specific codebase, for this class of change, is close to 100% historically. Treat this as near-certain, not merely possible, and budget for a dedicated concurrency-audit pass before Tier 2 ships, not as an afterthought.
2. **Deterministic orchestration encoding wrong assumptions more rigidly — real.** A fixed goal-state enum (proposed/ready/running/waiting/blocked/completed/rejected) is a real commitment about what states are possible; a partially-completed F1/F2/F3 self-edit pipeline, or a task blocked on a relay message that itself timed out, may not map cleanly onto any single state, and a rigid state machine will force a wrong answer where a flexible LLM would have at least noticed the ambiguity.
3. **Procedure ossification — real, roadmap defers the mechanism.** "Expiry/retest rules" are named as a requirement but no concrete trigger (e.g., environment hash mismatch, dependency version drift, or a fixed re-test cadence) is specified anywhere in Tier 3. Monitorable, not fatal, but currently underspecified.
4. **Outcome optimization narrowing Echo toward benchmark competence at creative/personal cost — real and directly named in Codex's own §17 note** ("preserve persona where it serves the user") — correctly flagged as a risk, not yet designed against with a concrete counter-metric (e.g., a held-out personal/creative task battery re-measured after any Tier-1+ change, to catch regression in exactly the dimension this concern names).
5. **Routing optimization starving exploration — real, already has a partial answer in this codebase** (`UNDER_SAMPLED_REFRESH_PROBABILITY`, `fair_sample_refresh` — an existing, real mechanism from this project's own history for exactly this failure mode in the current router). Any new outcome-trained router should inherit or re-derive an equivalent floor, not assume clean exploitation is safe by default.
6. **Held-out tests becoming de facto training data through repeated reuse — real, and E1's design doesn't yet name a re-use limit** (how many times can the same hidden-test set be run against new candidates before it stops being "held out" in any meaningful sense?). This should be a stated parameter, not implicit.
7. **Self-model confidence becoming another proxy to optimize — real, Goodhart risk**, and this project's own §8 (Codex's report) already found one live example (the `world_model.accuracy` field, which is actually an inverse-surprise statistic mislabeled as accuracy) — a concrete, already-observed instance of exactly this failure mode, not a hypothetical.
8. **Append-only evidence becoming operationally unusable at scale — real, and already measurably happening.** 128,837 memory entries, 21,382 fresh interaction rows in one sample window; this project has already had to build multiple log-rotation/retention mechanisms (Finding 51's "metabolism" work) reactively after the fact. A Tier-0 evidence ledger with per-attempt full provenance will grow faster than any existing log this project has, and no rotation/summarization strategy is specified for it.
9. **Provenance overhead consuming enough context to negate gains — real, and this project has a specific, exact precedent for it.** Finding 53 (this repo's own history, 2026-07-21): a synthesis system-prompt leaked verbatim into a user-facing response because a token-budget calculation didn't account for a newly-added fixed-size block (the Modelfile identity prepend). A richer per-attempt provenance/evidence-manifest requirement is exactly the kind of new fixed-overhead block that has already caused this specific failure once in this codebase. This is not addressed anywhere in the source report.
10. **Task IDs creating false boundaries around causally connected work — real**, especially for multi-turn conversations where a "task" might legitimately span several exchanges; no cross-task credit-linking mechanism is specified.
11–14: speculative/architecture-design questions not yet answerable from source inspection alone — correctly left as open design questions, not attackable with current evidence.
15. **What prevents a model from indirectly manipulating its evaluator through artifacts or context — genuinely unresolved.** Nothing in the current design prevents a generated artifact (a docstring, a comment, a log message) from containing text that later influences an LLM-judged evaluation of a *different* artifact, via ordinary retrieval — this project has already found exactly this class of false-positive twice in unrelated contexts this same week (a docstring's own example text matching its own detector's regex, twice, per this repo's Findings 63 and 83 and the §3 false-positive found in this very review).

**Classification:** #1 (concurrency) and #9 (provenance overhead) are **repairable before implementation, but only with dedicated engineering attention this roadmap doesn't currently budget for**. #7 and #15 (proxy-gaming, evaluator manipulation via artifacts) are **monitorable after implementation** — hard to fully prevent in advance, worth instrumenting for detection. #2, #3, #5, #6, #8, #10 are **repairable with explicit design choices not yet made**. None are fatal to the architecture as a concept.

## 16. Roadmap/dependency challenge

The proposed Tier ordering (0: contract/metrics → 1: retrieval/synthesis-baseline → 2: durable goals/peers → 3: procedural memory/routing → 4: self-improvement service → 5: model/backend) is broadly defensible, with one clear correction:

**E6 (model/backend comparison) should move earlier, not stay at Tier 5.** Codex's own §13 explicitly states "E3/E6 must test whether improving the model alone beats orchestration changes; the investigation does not prejudge that result" — but the roadmap then places E6 after three tiers of orchestration investment (Tiers 1–3) that would all be **wasted effort if E6 later shows the base model was the dominant constraint all along.** The mission brief's own question ("Ask whether E6 should happen earlier because a model swap might dwarf orchestration gains") is correct to raise, and this review's answer is: **yes, run a cheap version of E6 (the "compare installed specialist models" half, which requires no new downloads) at Tier 0, in parallel with the outcome-contract work, before committing to Tier 1–3's orchestration investment.** This costs little (models are already installed) and directly de-risks the rest of the roadmap.

**Tier 0's Outcome/Attempt Contract does not require the eventual production task kernel to validate — correct as designed.** E1 can and should be built as a standalone, scratch-directory experiment harness, not as a component of Tier 2's durable executor. This separation is implicit in Codex's own description but should be stated as an explicit non-dependency, since conflating "build the measurement" with "build the production system that will use it" is exactly how a roadmap's early tiers balloon in scope.

**Durable goals (Tier 2) before procedural memory (Tier 3) is defensible** — a procedure has nowhere durable to be *applied from* without goal persistence, so the ordering holds. **No tier should be considered validated enough to build on until E1 (Tier 0) proves the evaluation methodology itself is trustworthy** — this is already the roadmap's own stated logic and this review agrees with it as written.

## 17. Required repairs before implementation

In priority order, the concrete fixes each experiment needs before it can license the claims it's designed for:

1. **E5**: add a measured embedding-distance floor between teaching and withheld tasks; specify procedure-authoring blindness to withheld variants; specify treatment of failed-teaching families (retain as zero-procedure control, don't drop); add family-scoped retrieval isolation.
2. **E1**: resolve task-vs-candidate statistical unit explicitly; add "historical pool selection bias" to the confound list; state whether retries count as independent candidates.
3. **E3**: build the condition-integrity manifest (call counts, actual prompts, TOOL-LIST delivery, synthesis-path taken) for every arm, not just the baseline — apply the exact verification method that caught the 2026-09-15 Condition-A failure.
4. **All experiments touching RiverBrain or the vector index**: apply and independently verify the Tier-8 redirect-before-first-access pattern per state path, confirmed against the real production file (zero bytes landed there), not assumed from reading the patch code.
5. **E4**: require at least one forced-replanning task (a genuine mid-execution environment change), not only checkpoint-resumption tasks.
6. **Provenance/evidence-manifest design**: budget its token/context cost explicitly against this project's own Finding-53 precedent before any Tier-0 field list is finalized.

## 18. Revised experiment recommendations

- Add **E8 — Longitudinal accumulation trial** (design sketched in §11): 3–5 of E5's families, re-measured at T0/T1/T2/T3 across real intervening days of normal operation, with unrelated procedures taught between measurements and no re-teaching of the tracked families. This is the only design in the program that can speak to Level 5 of the claims ladder, and it is currently absent.
- Reorder: run E6's "compare installed models" half at Tier 0, alongside the outcome-contract work, before Tier 1 orchestration investment begins.
- Treat E1 as a prerequisite gate for trusting *any other experiment's* outcome measurements — if E1 fails to show the hidden-test gate discriminates better than the heuristic, every other experiment's "independently scored outcome" metric inherits the same discrimination weakness E1 was built to diagnose.

## 20. Final verdicts

**V1 — Is "outcome validity and credit assignment" the highest-leverage bottleneck?** Yes, on the evidence presented, and this review's own findings reinforce rather than undermine it — nearly every gap found in E1–E5 above (candidate-pool selection bias, evaluator ancestry, retry independence, survivorship) is a variant of the same underlying problem: this codebase does not yet have a trustworthy way to know whether an outcome was actually better. V1: **CONFIRMED**.

**V2 — Is the outcome/attempt contract ready to design?** Mostly, with the gaps in §5 (retry independence, model-digest feasibility, unused `synthesis_integrity_log.jsonl` attribution mechanism) needing resolution first. V2: **PROCEED WITH REQUIRED REPAIRS**.

**V3 — Can E1 establish evaluator superiority?** Only after the task-vs-candidate unit is resolved and the historical-pool selection-bias confound is either measured or designed around (e.g., by generating a fresh, non-archived candidate pool rather than reusing the old gate's output). As specified, E1's result would be real but narrower than its own success criterion implies. V3: **PROCEED WITH REQUIRED REPAIRS**.

**V4 — Can the proposed E3 avoid this project's own prior condition-integrity failures?** Only if the condition-integrity manifest in §9 above is built and checked for every arm before any outcome is interpreted. The design's instinct (verify the baseline) is correct but incomplete — it needs to verify every arm, symmetrically, given that the actual 2026-09-15 failure was in a *labeled-direct* arm, not the baseline. V4: **PROCEED WITH REQUIRED REPAIRS**.

**V5 — Can the proposed E4 meaningfully test bounded autonomy?** Not as specified — without at least one forced-replanning task, E4 cannot distinguish genuine adaptive autonomy from workflow replay, which is the exact distinction the mission brief asked this review to protect. V5: **DO NOT IMPLEMENT YET** (specifically: do not treat a passing E4 as autonomy evidence until the forced-replanning stratum is added).

**V6 — Can the proposed E5 establish reusable competence?** Partially. Its model-prior and retrieval-advantage controls are genuinely good. Its semantic-duplication, procedure-leakage, family-contamination, and survivorship gaps are real and currently open. Even fully repaired, E5 alone establishes Level 4 (Transfer), not Level 5 (Accumulation) — no amount of repair to E5 as scoped closes that gap; it requires E8. V6: **PROCEED WITH REQUIRED REPAIRS**, with the explicit caveat that "repaired E5" is still not "accumulated competence."

**V7 — Do the seven experiments collectively establish cumulative competence growth?** No. This is the central finding of this review (§1). An additional longitudinal experiment (E8, §11/§18) is required, and does not currently exist in the roadmap. V7: **ADDITIONAL EXPERIMENT REQUIRED**.

**V8 — Implementation readiness overall:** **PROCEED WITH REQUIRED REPAIRS.** Not "do not implement" — the diagnosis (V1) is sound, and Tier 0's outcome-contract work is genuinely buildable as a standalone, low-risk artifact per §16. But nothing in Tiers 1–4 should be built against the current specifications of E1/E3/E4/E5 without the repairs in §17, and the roadmap's own implicit promise of "cumulative competence growth" should not be claimed as validated by E1–E7 even after every repair — only E8 can speak to that, and it does not yet exist.
