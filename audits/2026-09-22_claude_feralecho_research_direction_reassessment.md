# FeralEcho Research Direction — Strategic Reassessment

**Method note.** This is a read-only assessment. No production code, Echo, RiverBrain, FAISS, council/routing, CLAUDE.md, or backups were touched to write it. I did not seek out, read, or infer Codex's parallel answer to this same prompt before writing or committing this file. Claims are labeled **OBSERVED** (verified in this repository's code or logged data, by me or by a prior session whose evidence I re-checked directly for this document), **INFERRED** (a reasoned conclusion from OBSERVED facts, not itself directly measured), or **SPECULATIVE** (a hypothesis with no current evidence either way). Where useful I name the discriminating experiment that would move something from SPECULATIVE to OBSERVED.

I was asked not to protect prior work from criticism because effort was already spent on it, and not to equate persistence, behavioral change, or complexity with progress. I've tried to hold to that, including about work from this same session.

## 1. Is persistent accumulated competence the correct next scientific target?

**Short answer: it's the right long-run aspiration, but it is premature as the *next* target, because at least two prerequisite capabilities the target depends on are currently unbuilt or shown weak, and one is already demonstrated working in narrow form.** Aiming the whole research program at "accumulated competence" right now asks a system that hasn't yet shown reliable single-shot induction from a handful of examples (AP-0's own qualification result, OBSERVED this session) to also show *compounding* improvement over many such steps. That's building the second story before the first one is load-bearing.

More precisely, "accumulated competence" bundles at least three separable claims, and FeralEcho is at a different maturity level on each:
1. **Can the system retain a validated change across a process boundary and have it affect future behavior?** OBSERVED, already yes, in a narrow but real form — RiverBrain's per-`(model, task_type)` online statistics persist to disk, reload on restart, and demonstrably change future model selection (Finding 10's fix, and Finding 39's tag-boost/fair-sampling verified live against real selection outcomes). This is small, but it is a real instance of experience → evaluation → retained state → behavior change, already running continuously in production.
2. **Can the system reliably *induce* a new piece of procedural knowledge from a small number of examples, when that knowledge isn't just "which of N known options performed best"?** OBSERVED this session (AP-0 qualification): no, not from the local 7B-class models available, for anything beyond a direct lookup table (K1). A deterministic symbolic search recovers the same information trivially (this session's diagnostic), which rules out "the information wasn't there" and leaves "the local model can't reliably do this specific kind of inference" as the standing explanation.
3. **Does the system *improve* over repeated cycles of (1) applied to (2)-shaped content, i.e. does competence compound?** Not directly tested by anything in the repository yet. The closest existing data — self-edit's ~2-month convergence history — does not show it (see §4 and §7).

Given (2) is currently a hard wall and (3) has never been tested, "accumulated competence" as a single research target conflates a solved prerequisite, an unsolved prerequisite, and an untested claim. The more useful reframing, argued through the rest of this document: **the immediate target should be extending (1) — the mechanism already proven to work — into richer content, rather than trying to force (2) to work well enough to build (3) on top of it.**

## 2. Is AP-0 on a plausible path, or a distraction?

**Both, depending on which half of AP-0 you mean.** The *qualification discipline* AP-0 introduced — freeze before running, verify the instrument against known-answer mutants before trusting it, require an independent evaluator, refuse to move to the next stage until the current one qualifies — is genuinely valuable and reusable, and this session's diagnostic (a deterministic solver, itself mutation-tested) is a direct product of that discipline. I'd keep that machinery.

The *specific bet* AP-0 makes — that an LLM constructor, given a handful of synthetic episodes in an invented micro-world, can reliably perform genuine relational/inductive reasoning — has now been tested twice (QA and the pre-declared QB contingency) and failed both times on exactly the two conventions (K2 ordering, K3 operation-table) that require real induction rather than direct observation. This session's diagnostic closes the door on the most charitable remaining excuse ("maybe the episodes just don't contain enough information") — they do, provably, and a few dozen lines of brute-force search recovers them instantly. That leaves "the local model can't do this reliably" as the standing, evidence-backed explanation, not a hypothesis still open.

Continuing to refine AP-0's Stage 1 harness (bigger episode sets, different representations, another constructor) chases a capability ceiling in the wrong layer: no amount of harness engineering fixes a base-model reasoning limitation, it can only narrow the specific cases where the limitation doesn't bite. That's effort spent perfecting the measurement instrument for a phenomenon (in-context LLM induction on toy conventions) that this session's own results suggest isn't the actual bottleneck standing between FeralEcho and accumulated competence — see §5.

## 3. What is FeralEcho already doing well that may be undervalued or mismeasured?

- **RiverBrain's online per-`(model, task_type)` learning (OBSERVED).** This is a real, live, verified instance of exactly the causal shape the whole accumulated-competence question is asking about — experience (a real quality signal from sandbox pass/fail, conversational scoring, or now blended council ratings) → evaluation → retained numeric state → behavior change (model selection) — running continuously with genuinely large sample sizes (coding: 29,140 observations; personal: 18,449; per Finding 3's re-verification). CLAUDE.md's own framing of this treats it mechanically, as "model scoring infrastructure," rather than naming it as what it structurally is: a small, working proof that FeralEcho *can* accumulate competence, in the narrow domain of "which model is good at what." I think this is under-recognized as a template for what a working next step should look like, precisely because it's unglamorous next to synthetic-world experiments.
- **The Liveness Ledger's discipline (OBSERVED, 41 checks as of the last count in this repo).** "Does this actually have a genuine, externally observable effect, independent of its own self-report" is exactly the epistemic standard any future learning claim will need to survive. This project already has a working, extensible mechanism for enforcing it. That's a real asset for the next research program, not just defensive infrastructure.
- **The self-edit safety pipeline (F1/F2/F3) plus the fitness gate (Finding 19).** A genuine "don't retain unless it's at least as good" check exists and works — the gate correctly rejects most candidates. This is a real, if binary and coarse, retention filter.

## 4. What fundamental capability is actually missing?

Not one thing — the evidence points at a combination, and I don't think forcing a single diagnosis would be honest:

- **Retention** — largely solved at the engineering level (self-edit's F1/F2/F3 + fitness gate; RiverBrain's persistence; AP-0's own designed-but-never-needed carrier-hash/fresh-process protocol). Not the bottleneck.
- **Evaluation / grading** — solved for pass/fail-shaped tasks (real sandboxed hidden tests, oracle mutation-testing, nonce-graded output). Weak for anything graded, since RiverBrain's mean is a single scalar per `(model, task_type)` with no sub-component breakdown.
- **Induction** — the clearest, most directly demonstrated gap (AP-0, this session). Local 7B-class models reliably fail relational/inductive reasoning over a handful of examples even when the information is sufficient.
- **Consumption/execution of an already-correct procedure** — a *second*, independently demonstrated gap, distinct from induction: AP-0's QA gate data shows the hand-written, verified-correct K2 procedure is used correctly by the worker only 2 of 4 times (OBSERVED, this session's diagnostic report). Even a perfect inducer would run into this.
- **Credit assignment below the whole-artifact level** — missing entirely. Every retention decision in this codebase is binary (accept/reject a whole draft, whole self-edit candidate, whole procedure). Nothing retains the correct 80% of a partially-right artifact and revises only the wrong 20%. I think this is a more consequential, and less discussed, gap than induction itself — see §8.
- **Abstraction formation** — not attempted anywhere in the codebase in a genuine sense. What exists either memorizes (the historical dream-cycle self-quoting loop, Finding 11; reflection_shard's measured 15% near-duplicate rate even after being fixed once, Finding 78) or performs narrow, pre-enumerated pattern-matching (AP-0's four-operation hypothesis space, task-type classification). Nothing forms a genuinely new category or generalization that wasn't already enumerated by a human designer.
- **Metacognition** — partial. `self_model.json`, the Liveness Ledger, and `echo_ground_truth.py`'s slices give the system accurate, ground-truth-checked facts about its own *structure* (is self-edit gated, what's my current valence). They do not yet give it a reliable way to check a *specific claim it's about to make* against evidence before making it — `self_knowledge_verification.py` (Finding 45) is a narrow pattern-matcher for three known false-claim shapes, not a general check, and Finding 43 showed confabulation compounding across turns despite this machinery already existing.
- **Autonomous curriculum generation** — exists nominally (`curiosity_engine`, `garden_manager`, `echo_projects`' autonomous loop) but is not measurably anchored to the system's own weak points. The physiology audit (Finding 75, OBSERVED) found several of the signals that would need to drive a genuine "practice where I'm weak" loop are running on near-saturated or near-constant real ranges.
- **Self-modification of the learner itself** — entirely absent. Self-edit modifies application code; nothing in this codebase has ever targeted RiverBrain's own update rule, feature set, or windowing as an object of improvement. There is no meta-learning loop.

If I had to rank these by leverage for the next step: **credit assignment below whole-artifact granularity** and **extending the one proven working mechanism (RiverBrain-style online statistics) into richer content** are the two highest-leverage gaps to close next, ahead of "make induction work better," because induction is being asked to do too much work in a system with no way to partially credit a near-miss.

## 5. Frozen model vs. system-level learning — both directions of the question

**Are we trying to make a fixed pretrained model "learn" in a way its architecture doesn't support?** For AP-0's K2/K3 specifically: **INFERRED, yes, partially.** In-context relational induction from a handful of examples is a known-weak capability for 7B-class open models generally, not a FeralEcho-specific bug. This session's diagnostic makes the case directly: the information is provably sufficient (a formal, mutation-tested search recovers it every time), so the gap sits in the model's inference, not the harness or the evidence. Continuing to ask the *same class* of model to do the *same class* of task, with a better prompt, is asking an architecture to do something its scale doesn't reliably support — this is not a claim about frozen weights being disqualifying in principle, it's a claim about what a 7B model's in-context reasoning demonstrably does and doesn't do well, evidenced twice now (QA and QB).

**Conversely, are we too quick to dismiss system-level learning because weights don't change?** **INFERRED, no — if anything the opposite bias shows up in this project's own documentation.** RiverBrain's online statistical learning already satisfies "the causal change actually occurs, and it's the level where it occurs" — it is real learning at the system level, verified with real production data and real behavioral consequences, and CLAUDE.md's own text treats it as ordinary infrastructure rather than naming it as evidence that system-level (non-weight) learning already works here. I don't think this project needs convincing that non-neural learning counts; if anything it needs to notice more clearly that it already has a working example of it.

## 6. Alternative research programs (existing hardware, existing local models, existing architecture, ~zero additional cost)

For each: what could genuinely improve over time, what persistent state carries it, and how to distinguish real improvement from retrieval/config/compilation/contamination.

### Program A — Extend RiverBrain-shaped online learning to finer-grained, real content
Instead of trying to get an LLM to induce a *new symbolic procedure* from a few synthetic examples, extend the mechanism already proven to work: per-`(model, task_type)` scalar competence tracking → per-`(model, task_type, specific recurring failure signature)` tracking, using real, already-logged production data (sandbox failure messages, self-edit rejection reasons, council dissent categories — all already captured). What improves: routing/selection quality on genuinely held-out task instances, measured against real outcomes already accumulating. Persistent state: a direct, disclosed extension of `model_task_stats`. Discriminant: does routing accuracy on a held-out slice of real future tasks improve as more real observations accumulate, versus a fixed baseline that never updates? This is falsifiable against real production data with no new experiment needed to start collecting it.

### Program B — Symbolic induction over FeralEcho's own real operating history, using the machinery this session just built
This session's identifiability solver and mutation-testing discipline generalize directly: instead of an invented toy world, apply the same method to a real, enumerable question already answerable from existing logs — e.g., "under what real, logged conditions (recent MLX crash cluster, RAM pressure, time-of-day, recent liveness-ledger failures) does a self-edit attempt actually succeed or fail?" The hypothesis space (a small set of already-logged boolean/numeric conditions) is enumerable; the outcome (self-edit success/fail) is already recorded (`SELF_EDIT.log`, `self_edit_outcomes.jsonl`); nothing new needs to be generated. What improves: a genuinely deployable gating rule, validated the same way AP-0's diagnostic validated its K3 hypotheses — fit on a training window, checked against a **held-out, later** time window (not just a train/test split of the same period, which risks non-stationarity looking like generalization). Persistent state: a new, disclosed, human-reviewed gating condition (same review posture as every other self-edit gate change in this project's history). Discriminant against benchmark contamination: the test window must be *strictly later in time* than the training window, and the rule must be evaluated against outcomes that occurred after the rule was frozen, not just historically.

### Program C — Anchor autonomous curriculum generation to measured weaknesses instead of open-ended prompts
`curiosity_engine`/`echo_projects`' autonomous loop currently draws its practice material from philosophically-framed questions. A cheap, concrete redirection: feed it the Liveness Ledger's own near-miss/failure signal (e.g., `reflection_shard_generation`'s measured 15% near-duplicate rate, Finding 78) and let it generate practice attempts specifically targeting the measured weakness, then re-measure the same ledger check afterward. What improves, if anything: a specific, named, already-instrumented metric. Persistent state: none new — reuses existing logs. Discriminant: did the targeted metric measurably move, versus its own recent baseline variance, after the practice cycle — not "did the system produce more text."

### Program D — Finish the physiology audit before adding anything else
The highest-value *non-experiment* next step may be completing the recalibration/retirement pass Findings 75–80 already started: several Global-Workspace-era signals (coherence_tension, world_surprise, valence thresholds — partially recalibrated in Finding 77; `coupling_estimate` — still confirmed to have zero consumer) were built on the assumption of variance that measurement later showed wasn't there. Building a new accumulation mechanism on top of signals that are still saturated or unconsumed risks exactly the "architectural complexity mistaken for progress" trap. What improves: nothing new, deliberately — this converts existing dead/thin machinery into either a real signal or an honest removal. Discriminant: already built into the Liveness Ledger's own methodology (real variance in recent history vs. a fixed baseline).

## 7. Architectural dead ends — named directly, not softened

- **`coupling_estimate`.** Computed, persisted, given a self-report slice (Finding 78) — and still has **zero behavioral consumer** (OBSERVED, re-confirmed against the current codebase state described in CLAUDE.md). As currently wired, it cannot contribute to accumulated competence because nothing acts on it. Not a claim that the underlying idea is worthless — a claim that the current instance is inert.
- **Memory retrieval, as an accumulated-competence mechanism specifically.** The real ablation experiment (Finding 76, OBSERVED) found the effect of disabling retrieval on real `personal`-task quality was statistically indistinguishable from the experiment's own noise floor (p=0.48/0.37, n=30 vs n=5). Separately, direct code inspection (Finding 75) found memory retrieval touches no model-selection, task-classification, or self-edit-targeting path. Whatever memory retrieval is doing (and it may be doing something real for conversational continuity — this experiment's noise floor was too wide to rule that out), it is currently a dead end **specifically as a mechanism for accumulated competence**, because nothing downstream of it is measurably different when it's removed.
- **Pushing further LLM-only induction on invented micro-worlds (a "QC/QD/QE" constructor).** AP-0's own qualification protocol correctly declined to do this by design. I'm naming it explicitly as a dead end for the sub-goal of "make a 7B local model do reliable relational induction via better prompting alone" — not a claim about LLMs in general, a claim about the specific, already-twice-tested approach.
- **The hourly self-edit hourly hourly loop as a source of *improving* coding competence.** The convergence data across ~2 months of real operation does not show demonstrable improvement (Findings 16, 32, 52 all describe near-duplicate or non-convergent cycles across successive prompt families). The earlier "stuck at a constant 4/4 quality ceiling across 36 real deploys" reading has since been traced to a coarse scoring metric, not necessarily a genuine capability ceiling (Finding 91) — so this is *not yet* proven to be a dead end the way the two items above are; it's an open question the metric fix now makes measurable, but as of today there is no positive evidence of accumulating quality, only the absence of contradicting evidence for a plateau.

## 8. Re-examining the causal skeleton

The proposed chain — `experience → evaluation → retained change → boundary → novel transfer → measurable improvement → accumulation` — is a reasonable linear decomposition, but I think it's missing an explicit stage, and the omission matters more than any single link's reliability:

**`experience → hypothesis generation → evaluation → CREDIT ASSIGNMENT (what part was right?) → partial retention → boundary → novel transfer → measurable improvement → accumulation`**

Every retention decision currently in this codebase is binary at the whole-artifact level: accept or reject a whole self-edit candidate, accept or reject a whole AP-0 draft, whole council verdicts. Real accumulation over *many* cycles plausibly requires being able to say "this candidate got the operation right and the constant wrong" and revise only the wrong part, rather than discarding a near-miss entirely and starting over from the same prior. AP-0's own K3 evidence shows this concretely: QA's constructor got the "add" family right more often than the others (8/12 instance-count correct, this session's diagnostic) — a whole-artifact accept/reject scheme has no way to preserve "the add-family reasoning is fine, focus effort on multiply/set/subtract," it just rejects the whole draft and tries again from scratch. I'd treat inserting a genuine credit-assignment/partial-retention stage as a more important skeleton change than anything about the induction step itself.

## 9. What I would pursue next if AP-0 had never existed

Program B (§6): take the self-edit pipeline's own real, already-accumulating operating history and ask whether a simple, enumerable, symbolically-checkable rule about *when self-edit succeeds* can be induced and validated against a genuinely held-out later time window. This sidesteps the "toy world" criticism entirely (real data, real stakes, real deployment path already exists via the self-edit gate mechanism), reuses this session's own qualification discipline and identifiability-solver code, and — if it worked — produces something immediately actionable rather than another taxonomy of what a synthetic experiment couldn't prove.

## 10. Decision

**B — Keep AP-0, but demote it to a component test, while making a different program primary.**

Reasoning: AP-0 produced a genuine, rigorous, disclosed negative result (local 7B-class models fail relational induction from sufficient evidence, in a way a trivial symbolic search doesn't) plus reusable infrastructure (the qualification protocol, the identifiability solver, the mutation-testing discipline). That's real value, and abandoning it (**D**) or redesigning around a wholly different mechanism while discarding it (**C**) would throw away tools this reassessment itself recommends reusing (§6, Program B). But continuing to treat "can an LLM constructor induce an invented micro-world convention" as the *primary* path toward accumulated competence (**A**) is not supported by the evidence this session produced — the bottleneck it surfaced (local-model induction capability) is not one more rounds of harness repair will fix, and the target metric (Stage 1's P vs NEUTRAL contrast) was never going to test the thing that actually matters most right now: whether FeralEcho's *system*-level mechanisms, which already work in narrow form (RiverBrain), can be extended into richer content. **E** (another path) collapses into B once B is stated precisely as "keep the tools, change the primary subject matter to real system data" — I don't see a genuinely distinct sixth option worth naming separately.

## IF THIS WERE MY PROJECT

I would stop adding new machinery for a cycle and do two things, in order. First, finish what Findings 75–80 already started: go through every Global-Workspace-era signal and either recalibrate it against real current variance or retire it, so the next thing built sits on inputs that actually move (§6, Program D). Second, run Program B exactly as described in §6/§9 — take the self-edit pipeline's own two months of real operating history, apply this session's own qualification discipline (freeze the analysis plan, hold out a genuinely later time window, mutation-test the solver before trusting it) to the question "can a simple, enumerable rule about when self-edit succeeds be induced from real data and validated against outcomes that hadn't happened yet when the rule was frozen." That single experiment would tell you something AP-0 structurally cannot: whether FeralEcho, using its own real history rather than an invented world, can produce a validated, deployable improvement to itself. If it works, it's a second real instance of accumulated competence (after RiverBrain) and a template for a third. If it doesn't, that's just as valuable to know before investing further, and it would do so on real stakes rather than a toy world's proxy.

## THE QUESTION WE SHOULD BE ASKING

**Not** "can FeralEcho learn an arbitrary invented convention from a handful of examples" — this session's evidence says no, for a reason (local-model induction) that better harness engineering won't fix. The question that actually discriminates the next useful step from another elaborate instrument: **"Using only mechanisms already proven to work here — real per-outcome credit assignment persisted as retrievable state, exactly the shape RiverBrain already demonstrates — can FeralEcho induce a genuinely new, validated rule from its own real operating history, and does that rule keep being right once time moves past the moment it was frozen?"** That question is answerable with existing data, existing hardware, and no invented world, and a real answer to it — either direction — would tell you more about whether "accumulated competence" is a reachable near-term target than another round of AP-0 possibly could.
