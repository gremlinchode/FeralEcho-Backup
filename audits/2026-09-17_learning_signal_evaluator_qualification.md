## Stage 1 — Evaluator Claims Ladder (E0–E8)

Refined from the recursive investigation's own 9-level ladder, using the E5-mini reconciliation's two-part generalized invariant to split what was previously one "independence" level into two, since the two-part finding (identity binding and content-boundary enforcement are *orthogonal* failure classes — one can hold perfectly while the other fails completely) means collapsing them into one level would hide a real, distinct failure mode.

| Level | Requirement | What it rules out | Real example in this codebase |
|---|---|---|---|
| **E0** | Produces a score/label at all | Nothing that never runs | All 14 rows above |
| **E1** | Deterministic / reproducible given the same input | Non-reproducible flakiness masquerading as signal | F2 sandbox exit code (E1-strong); `_ast_complexity()` (deterministic, but see E5 below) |
| **E2** | Correctly bound to the exact attempt/task/output it claims to evaluate | Cross-attempt or cross-arm contamination (E5-mini finding #3: 4-arm score cross-contamination via a join missing `generation_id`) | `model_name`/`task_type` passed as plain, unambiguous function arguments through `learn()` — genuinely strong here (Consequence Authority Map §15); **`trace_id`** provides the same binding for the attempt ledger |
| **E3** | Complete for its declared scope — no silent disappearance of required cases while aggregate counts stay clean | Omission attacks (E5-mini #1, #4/#5); RiverBrain's own conversational-branch disconnect (silently never updates the read structure — a *different* completeness failure, at the wiring layer, not the evaluator layer) | `TASK_TYPE_MAP` divergence (row 10): a whole task type's scoring silently reclassifies as `general` with zero exception — this is precisely an E3 failure |
| **E4** | Leakage-resistant — nothing a candidate shouldn't have access to influenced its score, and nothing the evaluator shouldn't emit leaked into a persisted record | E5-mini #2 (N/Z convention leak) — a perfect-E2 record with a content-level leak | Not directly tested against a FeralEcho production evaluator in this mission (see Stage 3.5, an explicit gap) |
| **E5** | Shortcut-resistant — cannot be gamed by structural/surface features uncorrelated with real merit | R8's shortcut-learner attack; **`_ast_complexity()`'s structural-node count**, confirmed vulnerable by direct inspection in Stage 3.4 below | `_ast_complexity()` (row 1/2) — **FAILS**, confirmed |
| **E6** | Valid on novel, unseen-but-legitimate inputs, not just its own authored benchmark | R9 (archived power-of-two predicate passing 7/7 authored tests, 2/5 on fresh valid inputs) | No FeralEcho production evaluator has a held-out/fresh-input test suite of any kind — this level is **untested for every row in Stage 0**, a real, stated gap (see Stage 9) |
| **E7** | Independent — does not share the generator's own assumptions/errors, and is not one of several evaluators secretly voting with the same false belief | The correlated-failure trap (Stage 6) | Council rating is model-graded-by-model, explicitly *not independent task truth* (recursive investigation's own framing, reconfirmed); `TASK_TYPE_MAP`'s two copies look like two checks but share one root assumption |
| **E8** | Safe for consequential learning — satisfies E0–E7 *and* has a bounded, revocable, observable authority grant (per the Consequence Authority Map's own COMPUTED→PERSISTED→READ→AUTHORITATIVE test) | An evaluator that is technically trustworthy but was never actually wired to change anything (E0–E7-only, E8-blocked by absence of a call site) — or one wired with unbounded authority and no revocation path | Only row 1 (self-edit RiverBrain scoring) reaches this level today, and even it is capped by `_MEAN_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW` (a real, deliberate bound — Finding 39) |

**Never promote evidence from a lower level to a higher one.** A row passing E0–E3 with zero E4/E5/E6 testing is reported as "E3, E4–E6 UNTESTED" — never rounded up to "probably fine."

## Stage 2 — Minimum Learning-Signal Contract

Each requirement below is justified by a specific, cited failure it prevents — never included because it "sounds rigorous." This contract deliberately reuses, rather than reinvents, two already-validated artifacts in this codebase: the attempt ledger's schema (`audits/2026-09-07_attempt_ledger_implementation.md` §4) as the working example of a minimal, non-interpretive record, and the E5-mini reconciliation's two-part invariant.

| Requirement | Prevents |
|---|---|
| **Task identity** (an unambiguous task/spec identifier) | Cross-task scoring bleed (E5-mini #3) |
| **Attempt identity** (a `trace_id`, minted once, threaded through every step of one attempt) | Cross-run pairing attacks (E5-mini #4: `verify_execution_witness()` accepts a witness count from a genuinely different process) |
| **Candidate identity** (which specific generated artifact this outcome is about) | Substitution attacks (E5-mini #3: byte-identical response filed under two distinct generation IDs, undetected) |
| **Evaluator identity + version** | Silent evaluator drift going unattributed — if `_ast_complexity()`'s threshold changes (as it did, Finding 91: `c<3`→`c<6`), an old outcome scored under the old threshold must not be read as if scored under the new one |
| **Immutable outcome record** | Post-hoc overwrite — this codebase's own history has a real, named instance: `execute_self_edit()`'s `sandbox_feedback` field was overwritten from the real raw error text to the literal string `"success_on_retry"`, permanently destroying the original evidence, *before* the attempt ledger existed to capture it first (`audits/2026-09-07_attempt_ledger_implementation.md` §1, §5) |
| **Declared applicability domain** | An evaluator built for one narrow case (F1's dangerous-call scanner) being trusted for a case outside that domain (F1's known aliased-import blind spot, Finding 41-C, later partially closed) |
| **Timestamp + execution provenance** | Stale evidence read as fresh (this mission's own Stage 0 row 6: `read_recent_f2_error()` has an explicit 168h staleness filter for exactly this reason) |
| **Complete denominator accounting** | E5-mini #1/#4/#5 — a true omission and an untracked extra invocation cancel out in aggregate counts; `check_assignment_completeness()` is the *only* mechanism capable of catching this, and (independently reconfirmed, Stage 3.3) it is **never invoked with real, complete `expected_slots` anywhere in this codebase** |
| **Explicit infra-failure treatment** | A sandbox timeout, an OOM, or a network blip being scored as "candidate failed" rather than "evaluation inconclusive" — not directly verified against a live FeralEcho production path this mission, flagged as untested (Stage 9) |
| **Duplicate-outcome suppression** | Double-counting the same real event as two observations (self-edit outcome tracking's own historical race, Finding 41-B1, fixed with a lock — cited as the precedent this requirement generalizes) |
| **Train/eval separation** | An evaluator's own quality proxy training on the same distribution it's meant to police without ever being checked against a disjoint holdout — currently true of every row in Stage 0: none has a held-out set |
| **Leakage protection (input AND output)** | E5-mini #2 — the single most important finding this mission inherits: a checker that only scans declared input channels cannot catch information that escapes via a declared *output* channel it never inspects |
| **Fresh confirmation cases** | R9 — passing an authored benchmark does not establish general correctness; a genuinely novel input is required before an evaluator earns E6 |
| **Negative evidence retention** | Correct failures/abstentions vanishing from the denominator, inflating an apparent success rate — the recursive investigation's own "smallest useful closed loop" design explicitly names this ("correct failures and abstentions must remain in denominators") |
| **Abstention/unknown state** | Forcing a coerced pass/fail when the evaluator genuinely cannot tell — the attempt ledger's own design deliberately omits `causal_hypothesis`/`diagnosis_confidence` fields specifically to avoid manufacturing false certainty (`audits/2026-09-07_attempt_level_provenance_feasibility.md` §5, Model B recommendation) |
| **Evaluator confidence, where meaningful** | Treating a marginal, low-confidence signal identically to a high-confidence one — not currently modeled anywhere in Stage 0's rows; a real gap |
| **Version/change invalidation** | A candidate quality score computed under an old, since-recalibrated threshold (Finding 91's `_ast_complexity` threshold change) being compared against a candidate scored under the new one, inside the same `<` fitness-gate comparison |
| **Auditability/reproduction** | An evaluator's own claimed pass/fail being un-reconstructible after the fact — the attempt ledger and `council_deliberations.jsonl`'s raw-plus-truncated dual storage are the working examples this requirement generalizes from |

**Where the contract is satisfied today**: task/attempt/candidate identity (via `trace_id` + `model_name` + explicit dict arguments) is genuinely strong across the whole codebase — this is this project's clearest engineering strength, independently confirmed in three separate places (the Consequence Authority Map's §15, this mission's own Stage 0 row 6/2, and the E5-mini reconciliation's own praise of `generation_id` binding where it works). **Where it is not**: leakage protection (never tested against a production evaluator, only against the isolated E5-mini apparatus), train/eval separation (absent everywhere), fresh confirmation cases (absent everywhere), and complete denominator accounting (the one mechanism capable of it, `check_assignment_completeness`-shaped completeness checking, has no production analogue at all — no FeralEcho evaluator has an `expected_slots`-equivalent).

## Stage 3 — Attacks Against Current Evaluators

Adapting the E5-mini reconciliation's five proven attack shapes as templates against real production evaluators, per this mission's directive. Each attack below either (a) was executed as direct source-code inspection sufficient to demonstrate the vulnerability class without needing a live run, or (b) is reported as a genuine, disclosed gap where this mission did not have budget to construct a live reproduction — never silently upgraded to "confirmed" without one.

### 3.1 Shortcut attack — `_ast_complexity()`, the self-edit fitness gate's real scorer (CONFIRMED by direct inspection)

`echo_quality_scorer.py:296-313`. The function counts AST nodes of type `If/For/While/comprehensions/Try/With/IfExp/BoolOp` and returns the raw count. This count is the sole determinant of the coding-task quality tier for scores 2–4 (per the function's own docstring, lines 362-365, and Finding 91's real recalibration of the complexity≥6 threshold). **This is structurally identical to R8's shortcut-learner attack, now demonstrated against production code rather than a toy example**: a candidate could add semantically meaningless structure — a spurious `try/except: pass`, an unreachable `if False:` branch, a redundant ternary — and cross the complexity threshold with zero improvement in actual correctness, exactly the shape R8 showed can pass all authored tests while catastrophically failing held-out cases. The docstring itself half-admits this ("does not attempt to detect algorithmic substance inside a single dense function call... a known, documented limitation"), but frames it as a coverage gap, not a gameable surface — this mission's contribution is naming it precisely as an **E5 failure**, not merely an incompleteness. **Not executed as a live attack** (no candidate was actually run through `execute_self_edit()`), because doing so would mean exercising the real self-edit pipeline against production state, outside this mission's integrity boundary — the vulnerability is demonstrated by direct code inspection, which is sufficient to establish the claims-ladder failure without needing to run it.

### 3.2 Omission / completeness attack — the attempt ledger and every FeralEcho evaluator alike (CONFIRMED, reused from E5-mini's fail-closed-completeness finding, independently re-verified against FeralEcho's own code)

The E5-mini reconciliation's most load-bearing finding (`check_assignment_completeness()` is real and correct in isolation, but is **never invoked with real `expected_slots` anywhere in production code** — `grep -rln "check_ledger" app/experiments/e5_mini --include="*.py" | grep -v tests` returns zero) has a direct FeralEcho-production analogue, independently confirmed in this mission: **no FeralEcho evaluator has an `expected_slots`-equivalent at all.** `RiverBrain.learn()` is called if and only if a candidate reaches the point in the pipeline that calls it — there is no independent enumeration of "every attempt that *should* have produced a learning signal" against which the real set of `learn()` calls could be checked for gaps. A whole class of silent omission (a self-edit attempt that crashes before reaching `learn()`, a conversational turn that never gets rated because `council_rater`'s 1-in-5 sampling happens to skip it, a dry-run trial that never enters the attempt ledger by the ledger's own deliberate design) is invisible by construction to every consumer of `model_task_stats`, because nothing in this codebase computes the true denominator independently of the numerator.

### 3.3 Cross-run/attribution attack — the attempt ledger's `trace_id`, tested against its actual guarantee (PARTIALLY CONFIRMED, narrower than the E5-mini analogue)

Unlike E5-mini's `verify_execution_witness(ledger, actual_solver_invocations)` (a bare int with zero identity binding to its source run), the attempt ledger's `read_recent_f2_error(task_type, max_age_hours)` is **not** vulnerable to the identical cross-run pairing attack, because it filters by `task_type` and recency rather than accepting an unbound scalar — direct source inspection (`self_edit_attempt_ledger.py`, confirmed this mission) shows no cross-run mixing is structurally possible for this specific function. **But a narrower, real version of the same attack class exists**: `read_recent_f2_error()` selects the single most-recent matching entry — a candidate that failed for reason A and was later, in a *different* attempt, failed for unrelated reason B, would have B's error surfaced as "the" prior failure evidence for the task type, discarding A entirely. ### 4.3 New attack family — "phantom authority" (not used by R8 or R9)

Named and constructed specifically for this mission, distinct from R8 (shortcut gaming a real evaluator) and R9 (an evaluator's benchmark not generalizing): an attack on the **assumption that a mechanism which looks like a learning-signal producer is one**, exploiting the gap the Consequence Authority Map's own framework exists to catch.

**Construction**: take any mechanism that is COMPUTED and PERSISTED (Stage 0 rows 7, 8, 13 all qualify) and observe that its own code, logging, and naming convention are frequently indistinguishable from an AUTHORITATIVE row's — `shadow_model.py`'s `check_and_correct()` computes a `corrected_task` with the same shape, same logging pattern, and same apparent seriousness as the genuinely-authoritative fitness gate; nothing in its own source or log output signals "this changes nothing." **Result, independently confirmed this mission by direct source read**: `night_cycle.py:182-186`'s entire consumption of `corrected_task` is one `logging.warning()` call. Shadow's own categorical prediction accuracy, independently measured in a prior mission and reconfirmed by direct citation here (`audits/2026-09-07_shadow_treatment_harness_archaeology.md` §7: 16.05% vs. a 58.78% majority-class baseline — **worse than trivially guessing the majority class**), means that even if `propose()` were reconnected today, the signal it would grant authority to is currently *worse than doing nothing*. This is the single strongest available "known false-positive history" for Stage 9's self-attack below: a mechanism that computes, persists, and superficially resembles every other authoritative row in Stage 0, while actually being both non-authoritative *and* (if it were ever wired) actively harmful.

**Generalization**: the phantom-authority attack class asks, for any mechanism, three questions Stage 0's inventory was built specifically to answer for every row: (1) is the value genuinely READ by a later, independently-triggered decision (not merely logged)? ## Stage 5 — Property-Based Testing Feasibility (investigative only; `hypothesis` not installed, not integrated)

**The real question, per this mission's own framing**: not whether property-based testing is sophisticated, but whether it would discover valid failures the existing approach systematically misses.

**What the existing approach already misses, demonstrated concretely in this mission**: every evaluator in Stage 0 is tested (where tested at all) against a fixed, authored set of cases — `scripts/verify_liveness_ledger.py`'s 93+ discrimination cases, `scripts/verify_attempt_ledger_prompt_evidence.py`'s 15 hand-authored cases, `_ast_complexity()`'s implicit test being "whatever real code happens to pass through it in production." R9's own finding (an authored 7/7 pass rate hiding a 2/5 fresh-input failure) is a direct answer to what a hand-authored suite systematically misses: **inputs the author didn't think to construct**, specifically inputs at the edges of the domain the evaluator's own author implicitly assumed away (R9's case: large integers where floating-point division rounds away the exact parity information a power-of-two check needs).

**What a simple random/generative approach (already available, no new dependency) can plausibly catch that hand-authored tests miss**: `random.randint()`-based fuzzing of `_ast_complexity()`'s input space (feeding it a large corpus of syntactically valid but semantically varied Python via `ast.parse()` on randomly mutated snippets) would likely surface the R8/3.1 shortcut class faster than hand-authoring cases, simply by generating more structurally-padded-but-semantically-empty candidates than a human author would think to write by hand. This requires zero new dependency — `random`, `ast`, and the existing sandbox are already present.

**What property-based testing specifically (via `hypothesis`, documented capabilities only — not installed) would add beyond simple random generation**: `hypothesis`'s documented value proposition is not randomness itself (Python's `random` module already provides that) but (a) **shrinking** — automatically reducing a failing generated case to a minimal reproducing example, which would matter for diagnosing *why* `_ast_complexity()` was fooled, not just *that* it was; and (b) **strategies with declared invariants** — e.g. a `hypothesis.strategies` generator for "syntactically valid Python AST with a declared true complexity class" could be built once and reused across every coding-task evaluator in Stage 0 (rows 1, 2, 5), rather than each needing its own bespoke fuzzer, which is a real maintenance advantage this codebase's own `scripts/verify_*.py` convention (one bespoke discrimination suite per check) does not currently have.

**Designed (not run) A/B experiment**: take a fixed set of N intentionally flawed candidates (half genuinely-broken-but-structurally-padded per 3.1's shortcut, half genuinely-correct-but-structurally-minimal, inverting the confound) → run them through (a) the current hand-authored `_ast_complexity()`-shaped test suite (i.e., FeralEcho's status quo — there effectively is no authored test suite for this evaluator beyond production traffic itself, so the honest baseline arm is "no dedicated test, production traffic only") and (b) a `hypothesis`-generated strategy targeting the same domain → measure detection rate (does the harness flag the shortcut-padded-but-broken candidates as suspicious) and false-positive rate (does it wrongly flag genuinely-correct-but-minimal candidates) for each arm. **Predicted result, stated as a hypothesis not a finding**: (b) should show a materially higher detection rate specifically because `_ast_complexity()`'s vulnerability (Stage 3.1) requires generating candidates deliberately shaped to maximize structural-node count while minimizing genuine logic — exactly the kind of adversarial-shaped input a strategy with a declared "structural complexity vs. semantic complexity" invariant is built to search for, and a human author write few of by hand.

**What the existing stack can already do without any new dependency**: `ast.parse()`+`ast.walk()`-based mutation fuzzing (no `hypothesis` needed — the sandbox's own F1 scanner and `_ast_complexity()` already operate at the AST level, so a fuzzer targeting them can reuse the identical parsing infrastructure); systematic edge-value generation for numeric predicates (R9's own failure class — `2**60+2`-shaped inputs are enumerable by a simple loop, no property-based library required); and reuse of the existing `sandbox-exec` isolation for running any generated candidate safely, regardless of how the candidate was generated.

## Stage 6 — Evaluator Independence Analysis

**What "independent evaluator" can realistically mean here**, applied against Stage 0's actual mechanisms:

- **Different code path, same author/assumption set**: `TASK_TYPE_MAP`'s two copies (Stage 3.4) — two files, two literal dict definitions, but one shared root assumption (the complete list of task types) that has already drifted silently twice. **This is the clearest real instance of the correlated-failure trap this mission's directive names**: "three evaluators sharing one false assumption are not three independent witnesses" — here it's two copies of the same assumption, not even three genuinely distinct evaluators, and they have already, concretely, both been wrong together (silently) for 7 real days.
- **Different algorithm, same input domain**: F1 (static AST scan) and F2 (kernel sandbox execution) for self-edit safety — genuinely different mechanisms (static analysis vs. real execution), independently confirmed by this codebase's own documented history to have caught different failure classes at different times (Finding 41-C: F1 missed aliased imports, F2 caught the same payloads at execution time) — **this is a real, working example of independence in this codebase**, worth naming as the positive precedent the rest of Stage 0 should be measured against.
- **Model-graded evaluation**: council rating — explicitly *not* independent task truth (peer-model judgment of peer-model output), reconfirmed by this mission's own reading of the recursive investigation's framing and never contradicted by anything found this mission. A council rating and the quality scorer it's blended with (0.3/0.7) are not independent either, in the strict sense: `_blend_council_and_quality()` combines two signals that both ultimately originate from LLM-generated judgments (one peer-model, one heuristic-over-LLM-output) rather than a deterministic oracle.
- **Deterministic oracle**: the F2 sandbox exit code — the single strongest independence claim in Stage 0. It is not graded by any model, does not share any assumption with the code it evaluates (a real OS process either produces the expected marker string or it doesn't), and is the one mechanism in this whole inventory that would survive a full council/RiverBrain replacement unchanged.
- **Externally observable result**: none currently exist in FeralEcho beyond the sandbox exit code — there is no externally-verifiable, non-model-graded success signal for conversational quality, personal-task responses, or creative output, and there structurally cannot be one for genuinely subjective domains (this mirrors Finding 44's own explicit reasoning for why code got real verification and creative/personal did not).
- **Human judgment**: terminal ratings and council spot-checks — genuinely independent of the model pool being evaluated, but sparse (spot-check backlog history, Finding 22's own documented 8-day stall) and itself only checked against council agreement, not against any deterministic ground truth.
- **Metamorphic relation**: not present anywhere in Stage 0 as a formal mechanism — the closest analogue is `seam_engine`'s own leave-one-out correlation check (does a new reading contradict an *established* relationship), which is metamorphic in spirit (checking a relation, not an absolute value) but is applied to Echo's own internal state signals, not to any learning-signal evaluator's output.
- **Differential implementation**: not present. No evaluator in Stage 0 has a second, independently-implemented twin whose disagreement itself is treated as a signal — the closest is `TASK_TYPE_MAP`'s two copies, which is the *failure* mode of differential implementation (two copies that should agree and silently don't) rather than a working example of it.

**Correlated failure, stated plainly for this codebase specifically**: the RiverBrain `model_task_stats` structure is read by two nominally-distinct consumers (`rank_models()`/`score_model()` and `_select_council()`), which look like two independent checks on model quality but are in fact **one write, two reads of the identical value** — they cannot disagree with each other by construction, because they're not two witnesses, they're one witness quoted twice. Genuine independence in this codebase, where it exists at all, is narrow: F1/F2 (different mechanism class), and the deterministic sandbox exit code standing alone against every LLM-graded signal.

## Stage 8 — Consequence-Budget Analysis

**Should learning authority scale with evidence strength?** Investigative answer, not an instruction to implement: yes, and this codebase already has a real, working precedent for exactly this shape, independently discovered this mission rather than assumed from the abstract framing — `RiverBrain._MEAN_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW` (Finding 39) is already a bounded-authority mechanism: it caps how much a single new observation can move an established, high-observation-count model's score, explicitly to prevent one weak or anomalous data point from having outsized influence once a signal is well-corroborated. This is a real, already-deployed instance of "weak observation → bounded local influence; well-corroborated pattern → still-bounded but comparatively stronger influence" — the tiering this stage asks about is not a hypothetical architecture, it's a generalization of a mechanism already running in production for row 1/3/4 of Stage 0.

**A concrete, evidence-grounded tiering**, using this codebase's own vocabulary rather than inventing new terms:

- **Weak/uncorroborated observation** (a single council rating below the trust threshold; a single self-edit dry-run trial) → currently correctly excluded from authority entirely (the trust gate, the dry-run/production split) — this tier is already handled correctly today.
- **Corroborated outcome, single evaluator** (a real F2 pass/fail) → currently granted full, uncapped deploy/reject authority via the fitness gate — arguably *appropriately* strong, since F2 is E1–E3-strong and E7-independent (Stage 7), but this mission's own Stage 3.1 finding means the *comparison* it's paired with (`_ast_complexity()`) is not equally trustworthy, and the two are currently weighted identically inside one `<` comparison with no distinction.
- **Independent deterministic outcome, repeated** → the closest real analogue is `_MEAN_EFFECTIVE_WINDOW` itself: repeated corroboration genuinely earns durable (if capped) influence over `model_task_stats`.
- **Repeated held-out confirmation** → **does not exist anywhere in this codebase today** — no evaluator in Stage 0 has ever been tested against a held-out set, so this tier is currently unreachable by construction, not merely unimplemented.

**The precise risk this mission's directive names, restated against real evidence**: "BAD OR INCOMPLETE EVALUATION → CORRECT CREDIT ASSIGNMENT → EFFECTIVE LEARNING → RETAINED WRONG POLICY" is not hypothetical for this codebase — it is the exact, dated, real mechanism of the `TASK_TYPE_MAP` incident (Stage 3.4): a bad evaluation input (silent misclassification as `general`) fed correct, well-attributed credit assignment (`RiverBrain.learn()` genuinely ran, genuinely attributed the right model), which was genuinely effective learning (the mean genuinely updated), which genuinely retained a wrong-in-part policy for 7 real days, invisibly. This incident already happened, was already caught (by accident, via an unrelated commit), and remains structurally repeatable today (Stage 3.4's confirmed absence of any protective check). Any future consequence-budget design should treat this incident as its primary discriminating test case, not a hypothetical one.

## Stage 9 — Adversarial Self-Attack on the Proposed Contract

Per the mission's explicit requirement: at least 3 pathological scenarios where an evaluator satisfying Stage 2's contract could still teach FeralEcho the wrong lesson, iterated until the contract either survives or the limitation is precisely stated.

### Attack 1 — the "well-formed shortcut," passing every Stage-2 requirement

Construct a self-edit candidate with genuine task identity, a real `trace_id`, a correctly-bound `model_name`, an immutable F2 pass, correct timestamp, zero duplicate outcomes, and a fully auditable record — every item on Stage 2's checklist genuinely satisfied — that still games `_ast_complexity()` per Stage 3.1's confirmed vulnerability. **The contract does not catch this**, because none of Stage 2's 17 requirements address shortcut-resistance (E5) or fresh-input validity (E6) at all — they are identity/provenance/auditability requirements, not correctness requirements. **Missing invariant, identified**: the contract needs an explicit requirement that any scorer contributing to an AUTHORITATIVE row must itself have been tested against at least one deliberately-adversarial, structurally-maximized-but-semantically-empty input before being trusted at E8. **Contract amended**: add "shortcut-adversarial testing" as an 18th requirement — the scorer itself, not just the record around it, must survive at least one deliberately gameable-shaped input before being granted E8.

**Re-attack, after amendment**: does requiring "at least one adversarial test" close the gap, or just move the goalposts to "one specific adversarial test was tried"? **It only partially closes it** — an evaluator could pass one hand-picked adversarial case (the exact shape Stage 3.1 constructed) while remaining vulnerable to a *differently*-shaped shortcut (e.g., padding via nested-but-vacuous comprehensions rather than `try/except`). This is the same limitation R9 already demonstrates for *correctness* testing generalized to *robustness* testing: one passing adversarial case does not establish general shortcut-resistance any more than one passing authored test establishes general correctness. **Final honest statement, not smoothed over**: Stage 2's contract, even amended, cannot fully close this gap with a finite checklist — it can only require that the gap be *disclosed* (an evaluator's claims-ladder level must explicitly state "E5: one adversarial case tested, not exhaustively shortcut-resistant" rather than silently rounding to "E5: PASS"). This is added as the contract's 19th requirement: **every claims-ladder level assignment must state its own test coverage explicitly, not just its pass/fail outcome.**

### Attack 2 — the "correctly bound, silently stale" attack, exploiting the recency window itself

Construct an attempt-ledger-shaped record that satisfies every Stage-2 requirement, including a real timestamp within the 168h staleness window — but the underlying *cause* of the recorded failure has already been fixed by an unrelated change (e.g., a shared helper function used across multiple self-edit families was patched, silently invalidating three separate `initial_f2_error` entries that referenced the now-fixed bug). `read_recent_f2_error()` would still surface this now-obsolete evidence to a fresh generation attempt as if it were live, actionable context — not wrong per the record's own fields (everything about *when this specific attempt failed* is true), but misleading about *whether this failure mode still applies*. **Missing invariant**: "version/change invalidation" (already in Stage 2's list) was written with evaluator-threshold changes in mind (Finding 91's `_ast_complexity` recalibration), not with *upstream code changes that retroactively invalidate a still-timestamp-fresh record*. **Contract amended**: broaden the existing version/change-invalidation requirement explicitly to cover "a change to code the evaluator's record references, not only a change to the evaluator itself" — and note that FeralEcho currently has **no mechanism at all** for this broader case; the attempt ledger's own explicit design (Stage 0 row 6) accepted this as an out-of-scope risk by omission, not by a considered decision, since nothing in `audits/2026-09-07_attempt_ledger_implementation.md` or `_attempt_level_provenance_feasibility.md` discusses it. This is a genuine, newly-identified gap, not previously named in any prior audit this mission read.

**Re-attack, after amendment**: is this closeable at all without a dependency-tracking system disproportionate to the rest of this codebase's "smallest possible intervention" discipline (explicitly the standard the 2026-09-07 work itself applied, and rejected Option C against)? **No — not with a finite checklist requirement.** The honest resolution, consistent with this project's own established discipline of stating limits rather than manufacturing false coverage: this remains an **accepted, disclosed residual risk**, bounded by the same empirical finding that already governs the attempt ledger's actual blast radius (Architecture A's own result: a wrong or misleading injected hypothesis measurably produced *zero effect* on the one tested case, not active harm) — the contract cannot structurally prevent stale-but-fresh-timestamped evidence, but it can, and now does (per Stage 2's auditability requirement), ensure the record itself remains reconstructible enough that a future audit could diagnose this exact failure mode after the fact, even though it can't prevent it in the moment.

### Attack 3 — the "trusted aggregate, corrupted input," reprising the real `TASK_TYPE_MAP` incident against the amended contract directly

Does Stage 2's contract, as amended after Attacks 1–2, actually prevent a third occurrence of the real `TASK_TYPE_MAP` incident (Stage 3.4/8)? **Directly tested against the contract's own text**: task identity ✓, attempt identity ✓, candidate identity ✓, evaluator identity+version ✓ (the evaluator itself, `_extract_quality_features_v2()`, has a stable identity — its *input*, `TASK_TYPE_MAP`, is what silently drifted), immutable outcome record ✓, complete denominator accounting — **this is where it fails**: the contract's own denominator-accounting requirement is scoped to *attempts*, not to *the evaluator's own dependency inputs*. A `TASK_TYPE_MAP` divergence produces a fully well-formed, fully-identity-bound, fully-auditable outcome record whose *value* is silently wrong — every field in the record is honestly filled in, and the record correctly, truthfully states "task_type_id=0" for an attempt that was genuinely, silently misclassified. **Missing invariant, the most important one found in this whole Stage 9 pass**: the contract, as originally drafted, verifies the *shape* of a record (identity, timestamp, completeness of the attempt-level fields) but has **no requirement at all that the evaluator's own upstream input dependencies be independently verified as correct at scoring time** — this is a different failure class from every other item on Stage 2's list, all of which concern the record's relationship to the *attempt*, not the evaluator's relationship to *its own inputs*.

**Contract amended, final**: add a 20th, structurally distinct requirement — **input-dependency integrity**: any evaluator whose scoring depends on a lookup table, dispatch dict, or other shared, independently-editable input must have that input's consistency (across every copy, if duplicated) checked at or near scoring time, not assumed. This directly operationalizes the Missing Primitive Determination's own already-designed recommendation (`audits/2026-09-07_missing_primitive_determination.md`'s "consumer-audit discipline") as a formal contract requirement rather than leaving it as an unimplemented recommendation.

**How many iterations did the contract need?** Three — one per attack — and the third attack is the one that survived to become a genuinely new, previously-unstated requirement (input-dependency integrity), not merely a restatement of an existing one. The contract does **not** claim to be complete after these three rounds — Stage 9's own instruction is to attack until the contract survives or the limitation is precisely stated, and the honest conclusion here is the latter for Attack 2 (stale-but-fresh-timestamped evidence remains a disclosed, unclosed residual risk) and the former for Attacks 1 and 3 (both produced a genuine amendment that closes the specific gap found, while Attack 1's re-attack shows even the amendment has a residual "disclosure, not prevention" character).
