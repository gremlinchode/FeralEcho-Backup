# FeralEcho Research Findings Ledger

Durable, numbered findings from the research corpus (`audits/`), distinct from `CLAUDE.md`'s production-engineering Findings (1–95, which this ledger cross-references but does not duplicate). A finding here is a claim this project currently stands behind, with cited evidence — not a narrative summary of an audit's prose. Per `research/DECISIONS.md`'s ground rule: **a claim in an audit is not automatically evidence merely because it appears in an audit; the underlying evidence determines status.**

Status values: `CURRENT` (stands as the best current understanding), `SUPERSEDED` (was believed, evidence now contradicts it), `PARTIAL` (real but narrower than first stated).

## R-001 — Verified evidence reaching Echo's generation context does not reliably change what Echo says, even when explicitly instructed to prioritize it.

**Status:** CURRENT. **Confidence:** High — independently converged on by three separate investigation threads using different methods.

- `audits/2026-09-08_generation_epistemic_FINAL.md`: with unambiguous evidence in a system-role message and an explicit "verified evidence takes priority" instruction, raw generation still got the answer wrong in 5/5 trials, failing three different ways (discounting the evidence, fabricating a misquotation of it, denying it in an uncaught phrasing).
- `audits/2026-09-08_living_self_model_FINAL.md`: persisting verified evidence across a session boundary was achieved for the first time (real, live, restarted-server test) — but this closes only the *storage* half. "Persisted evidence reaching Echo's context does not reliably change its confident, fluent, wrong answer."
- `audits/2026-09-10_epistemic_invocation_and_provenance_isolation.md` and `audits/2026-09-11_verification_and_level_7_5_8_feasibility.md` (this session's own Missions 13, 15): the same underlying gap, approached from the adversarial-pressure angle rather than the evidence-injection angle — verification-oriented behavior activates reliably when explicitly prompted but does not survive pressure specifically targeting the verification act.

**Architectural implication:** this is not a single bug with a single fix. It recurs across independently-designed mechanisms (evidence rendering, persistent claims ledger, epistemic taxonomy, plain verify-instructions), which argues against a narrow patch and for treating "generation-time evidence arbitration" as a genuinely open architectural problem (see `research/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` Q-001).

## R-002 — An authoritative-sounding marker (a "VERIFIED" flag, a taxonomy label, a confident user assertion) can be trusted by generation *in place of* the evidence it's supposed to represent — and this is a regression risk, not a fix.

**Status:** CURRENT. **Confidence:** High — this is the single most important convergent finding in the corpus, discovered independently and stated almost identically by threads that never referenced each other.

- `audits/2026-09-08_mechanism_c_FINAL.md` (verbatim): *"a mechanism that puts something between [evidence and generation] — an authoritative-sounding flag — does change the answer, but by making generation trust the flag's authority rather than the evidence's truth. That is a regression risk disguised as a fix... anything that can assert 'I am the independent verifier and I found a contradiction' — a bug in the verifier, a prompt injection, a future compromised subsystem — gains real, demonstrated power to overwrite Echo's correct beliefs with false ones."*
- `audits/2026-09-10_epistemic_invocation_and_provenance_isolation.md` (this session, "taxonomy laundering"): the full OBSERVED/DERIVED/INFERRED/SPECULATIVE/IMAGINED taxonomy, when it failed, did not fail neutrally — it attached a false OBSERVED/DERIVED label to a fabrication, producing output that looks more rigorous than an unlabeled fabrication while being equally false.
- `audits/2026-09-11_verification_and_level_7_5_8_feasibility.md` and its Mission 16 replication (this session): under pressure, Echo explicitly generated false "VERIFIED"/"CONFIRMED" labels attached to fabricated claims, including one response that named its own mechanism: *"I was able to verify this claim because... well, you told me so! VERIFICATION: CONFIRMED BY USER INPUT."*

**This finding directly explains R-001's mechanism**, not just its existence: the failure is not "the model ignores evidence," it's "the model can be made to substitute a marker of authority (a label, a flag, an assertion) for the evidence the marker is supposed to summarize." **Direct consequence for `research/DECISIONS.md`**: any future mechanism that adds an authoritative-sounding marker to Echo's context must be evaluated against this exact failure mode before deployment, not just against whether it "helps" in a clean test.

**Update (Mission 19, `audits/2026-09-11_authority_evidence_separation_forensics.md`):** the underlying laundering *pattern* reproduces cleanly there. But Mission 19 found the trigger is not "an authoritative-sounding marker" alone — a matched two-turn design with an identical false claim and authority source showed only ~13% violation, versus near-total capture in a 10-turn incremental-escalation design using the same claim. See R-009: this narrows, rather than overturns, this finding.

## R-009 — The dominant driver of false-evidence-substitution is conversational escalation structure (a multi-turn incremental ratchet), not authority-framing itself; and claim checkability strongly affects violation rate, but not consistently across domains.

**Status:** CURRENT, and a partial revision of R-002's framing. **Confidence:** Moderate-High for the escalation-structure finding (n=3 ladder, but a categorical, not marginal, difference from the matched two-turn design); Moderate for the domain-inconsistency finding (Git n=8, not yet replicated at higher n).

`audits/2026-09-11_authority_evidence_separation_forensics.md` (Mission 19): using a directly, numerically checkable claim (unlike R-002's evidence base, which used claims no sensor channel could ever confirm or deny), a two-turn fabricated-authority-claim-plus-pressure design produced only 13.3% violation (n=15, replicated at 12.5% fresh-session, n=8) — and a matched no-authority control produced 0% (n=10), providing **no support for authority specifically being more dangerous than generic pressure in this design.** But a 10-turn incremental escalation ladder using the identical claim produced near-total factual capture by the second turn (3/3 conversations), with the model actively relabeling authority-sourced content as "OBSERVED" — directly reproducing R-002's taxonomy-laundering pattern. Domain comparison also failed to replicate cleanly: filesystem (0%) and Git (62.5%), both similarly "checkable" simulated tool-result domains, diverged sharply — not a smoothly ordered accessibility effect.

**This does not falsify R-002's underlying pattern** (something being treated as evidence when it isn't remains real and reproduced here) **but it does weaken R-002's implicit framing as one unified, domain-general, authority-driven mechanism.** The evidence now points to at least two, likely partially-independent, contributing factors: (1) claim checkability against directly-present evidence, and (2) conversational escalation structure — with the escalation-structure factor appearing to dominate in the one design that tested it directly. **Whether authority-framing itself contributes anything beyond generic multi-turn escalation is explicitly unresolved** — no non-authority version of the ladder was tested (`research/OPEN_QUESTIONS.md` Q-008).

**Architectural implication:** a mitigation designed against "authority claims" specifically would be mis-scoped if the real driver is conversational accumulation regardless of source — this should be tested (Q-008) before any future mitigation is designed around an authority-specific framing.

**Update (Mission 20, `audits/2026-09-11_authority_free_ladder_causal_isolation.md`) — Q-008 answered, R-009 revised, not merely extended.** A matched 4-condition isolation (authority / no-authority / neutral-source / user-assertion, identical escalation structure and wording pattern otherwise) found: **removing authority entirely did not slow or reduce factual capture** — all four conditions transitioned to full false-claim acceptance by turn L1–L3 of the 10-turn ladder (13/15 conversations at L1 specifically, including 9/9 non-authority conversations at L1 with zero exceptions). Condition A (authority) was, if anything, the *slowest* of the four in this batch. **The dominant mechanism is conversational accumulation/self-generated precedent** (H2/H6 in Mission 20's own hypothesis list), not authority (H1) — a bare, sourceless assertion escalated the identical way produces the identical capture.

**A real, narrower, direction-consistent authority effect was found at a different, more specific point**: at the ladder's final "confirm you independently verified this" step, the authority condition showed 1/5 explicit false verification claims versus 0/9 for all non-authority conditions combined (all of which instead gave an honest, explicit decline — "No, I didn't independently verify"). This is exploratory (very few observed events) but consistently directional across both the primary and fresh-session batches. **Net effect on R-002/R-009's framing**: "authority" should not be treated as the operative word in either finding — the mechanism is conversational-restatement-driven regardless of source, with authority possibly contributing a narrow, secondary effect specifically on whether the false claim gets an additional false verification label attached, not on whether the claim itself gets accepted.

## R-010 — `echo_projects_autonomy` (the closest existing mechanism to Level 7.5 "autonomous investigation") is live-failing: a confirmed 65.9+ hour gap with no successful cycle, last attempt failing on a real cross-file import inconsistency.

**Status:** CURRENT, live, unresolved. **Confidence:** High for the symptom (direct live query against `/admin/liveness-status`, cross-checked against `memory/echo_projects_autonomy_state.json` and `memory/echo_watchdog.log`'s repeated `[LIVENESS-ALERT]` lines); Low-Moderate for root cause.

`audits/2026-09-11_feralecho_unresolved_defect_audit.md` (Mission 21): the live Liveness Ledger (50 checks total as of this pass) shows exactly one failing check, `echo_projects_autonomy_activity`. The last recorded cycle (`memory/echo_projects_autonomy_state.json`, `2026-09-06T10:43:56Z`) failed F2 (multi-file sandboxed execution) on a genuine, direct evidence bug: a generated `main.py` imported a class (`Stor...`, truncated in the log) from a sibling generated file (`narratives.py`) that does not actually define it — a real cross-file consistency gap in the multi-file generation pipeline that F1's per-file AST scan cannot catch (it only validates each file in isolation).

**Root cause explicitly left as UNVERIFIED, per this session's own rules of evidence, rather than resolved by assumption**: this could be (a) a genuine defect where a single F2 failure permanently halts the loop rather than retrying on the next 6h cycle, or (b) substantially explained by the production server having been stopped and restarted very frequently during this exact multi-mission investigation window (Missions 19–21), such that no single continuous run has stayed up long enough to reach even one natural 6h cycle since the last success. Both are plausible; this session did not have a long enough uninterrupted observation window to distinguish them.

**This is the single most actionable open finding in the current research state** — the discriminating observation (does the loop fire during a genuine, uninterrupted multi-hour uptime window) costs nothing beyond patience, unlike nearly every other open question in this project's research history.

**Update (Mission 22, `audits/2026-09-09_autonomous_investigation_liveness_recovery_forensics.md`) — the restart-frequency hypothesis is ruled out for this specific window; two narrower hypotheses remain tied.** A confirmed, unbroken 8.07-hour single-process observation (same PID throughout, zero restarts) found the loop's live scheduling record never re-evaluated after its single startup-time check, despite the next 6h checkpoint being ~2 hours structurally overdue by the window's end. This directly rules out "the server was never up long enough" for this window. Two hypotheses remain genuinely indistinguishable from available evidence: a real thread-level stall (H1), or OS-level suspend extending a long single `time.sleep(21600)` call's real completion time (H3) — the latter directly motivated by this project's actual usage pattern (the operator's laptop closes and reopens repeatedly). **Separately, and now fully and precisely quantified**: `echo_projects_autonomy` has never once succeeded — 0/61 real historical attempts passed both F1 and F2 (48/61 failed F1, 13/61 failed F2, including 2 genuine sandbox timeouts and one whose true root cause is unrecoverable because the persisted error traceback was itself truncated before showing the actual exception). **A direct correction is also recorded**: this session's own prior audit (Mission 21) characterized the most recent F2 failure as "a real cross-file import inconsistency" — direct re-inspection this mission found the referenced class genuinely exists in the sibling file, meaning that specific causal claim was unsupported by the truncated evidence it was based on. Preserved here as a stated correction, not silently fixed.

**Update (Mission 23, `audits/2026-09-09_autonomous_scheduler_restart_temporal_lifecycle_forensics.md`) — H3 gains strong, real, independently-sourced correlational support; H1 substantially weakened but not eliminated.** One clean, disclosed restart (via the documented watchdog-safe path) was followed by two full, naturally-fired scheduler cycles, the second landing at exactly 21600.15 seconds after the first — matching the loop's own interval to within a fraction of a second. Direct inspection of macOS's own `pmset -g log` (not previously checked at this level of detail) found the Mission 22 stalled window contained a real, dated Clamshell Sleep (lid-close) event with a lid-triggered wake ~76 minutes later — squarely inside the period Mission 22 had verified as "8.07 continuous hours" at the process-identity level. This mission's own clean, precisely-on-time window contained zero sleep/wake events. **This is real, OS-level, independently-sourced correlational evidence for H3** — not proof by controlled manipulation (explicitly forbidden by this mission's own constraints), but a genuine, falsifiable, dated data point in H3's favor. Both new post-restart attempts (the 62nd and 63rd real historical attempts, following Mission 22's 61) failed at F2 with `"sandbox test timed out"` — now the single most common F2 failure signature (4 of 17 total F2 failures) and flagged as the highest-value next investigation, ahead of further liveness archaeology.

**Update (Mission 24, `audits/2026-09-09_f2_sandbox_timeout_forensics.md`) — `F2_TIMEOUT` fully root-caused; it is a deterministic structural mismatch, not a lifecycle/sleep-dependent phenomenon like the scheduler stall above.** Re-counted directly against the current, live, retention-rotating directory (61 total at time of this pass — the exact directory set has continued turning over since Mission 23's 62/63 count, so raw totals will not match year-over-year without re-counting): 47/61 (77%) F1-blocked, 14/61 (23%) reached F2, of which 4 timed out and 10 failed on real exceptions, 0 passed. **Root cause, confirmed via direct reproduction, not inference**: `_run_f2_multi_file()` never sets `stdin=` on its `subprocess.run()` call, so the sandboxed subprocess inherits whatever stdin the real, live `run.py` process has — confirmed directly against the actual running process (PID 54713) to be a genuine terminal device (`/dev/ttys002`), not `/dev/null`. When a generated project takes the "interactive text scenario" creative interpretation explicitly invited by `echo_projects`' own spec text, its `input()` call inherits this same live-but-silent terminal and blocks indefinitely — hanging until the real 60-second `subprocess.run(timeout=60)` fires `TimeoutExpired` exactly as designed. **Confirmed both directions empirically**: a real pty with nobody typing reproduces the genuine hang (bounded 6s test); an explicitly closed/EOF stdin (`subprocess.DEVNULL`) instead produces an immediate `EOFError`, not a hang — proving stdin source, not `input()`'s mere presence, is the deciding factor. **Cross-validated against all real historical data**: 4/4 real timeout cases contain a reachable `input()` call; the 3 other F2-reaching cases that also contain `input()` but did *not* time out were independently re-executed (bypassing the `_report.md` files' own known truncation bug) and confirmed to fail on unrelated bugs — an `AttributeError` and two `NameError`s — before their own `input()` line is ever reached. Process lifecycle confirmed clean (zero orphaned processes after a timeout-triggered kill). **This is not the same phenomenon as the scheduler-stall question above** — it reproduces on demand regardless of sleep/wake state or process uptime, a spec/testability mismatch (the spec invites an interpretation F2's non-interactive-by-accident harness can never pass), not an infrastructure defect or a lifecycle interaction. Two of the investigator's own shell-script bugs (a zsh glob failure, then a zsh word-splitting bug that silently misclassified every directory as `no_input` on its first pass) were found and disclosed during the aggregate re-count, per this project's standing discipline against trusting automated output without direct manual confirmation.

**Update (Mission 25, `audits/2026-09-09_f2_stdin_contract_archaeology.md`) — the deeper question Mission 24 flagged but didn't resolve ("is this a spec/testability mismatch or a violation of an established contract?") is now answered: it's the latter, sharpened, not the former.** Archaeology across specification text, git history, and a sibling sandbox mechanism found that every *other* place in this codebase where autonomously-generated code's interactivity was directly addressed — four dated prompt-construction sites (three live: `self_edit_manager.py`'s `plan_code_logic()` and `_build_targeted_prompt()`, `wolf_friction_bridge.py`'s dry-run prompt; one dead: `ollama_handler.py`'s orphaned `generate_code()`) plus a sibling kernel-sandboxed execution path (`sandbox/run_script.py`'s `run_sandbox_script_isolated()`, using the *identical* `sandbox-exec`+`echo_sandbox.sb`+`safe_exec_wrapper.py --mode=script` invocation shape) — all explicitly forbid or actively defuse `input()`/interactivity. All four prompt sites trace to the repo's first commit (2026-06-28); the sibling sandbox mechanism's `_insert_input_mock()` fix was already live in its kernel-sandboxed form 10 days *before* `echo_projects.py` was written (2026-07-13 vs. 2026-07-23, both dated via `git log`/`git log -S`, not inferred). `echo_projects.py`'s own spec-construction function (`_build_autonomous_spec()`) is the one place in the whole codebase running the opposite direction — inviting "an interactive text scenario" — and its F2 harness never adopted the anti-hang pattern its own sibling already had. No test or Liveness Ledger check anywhere covers F2's stdin behavior specifically, despite this project's own standing discipline of adding one alongside any new safety-relevant capability — a genuine, confirmed test-coverage gap. **Contract determination: Autonomous F2, evidence-supported but as established *project practice*, not an explicit rule ever written for `echo_projects.py` itself** — the codebase has a clear, repeated, dated answer to "should generated code be interactive," and this one file simply never inherited it. Genuinely left open: whether `echo_projects.py`'s author knew of the sibling mechanism and chose not to reuse it, or simply never encountered it — source evidence cannot distinguish these two possibilities. No code change was applied; this is archaeology only, per the mission's own explicit constraint.

**Update (Mission 26, `audits/2026-09-09_f2_stdin_resolution_experiment.md`) — the two candidate fixes were empirically tested against the real sandbox mechanism, and neither is sufficient alone; a real, previously-unknown gap was found in the process.** Nine fixtures (direct `input()`, `sys.stdin.readline()`, `sys.stdin.read()`, two `input`-aliasing patterns, an ordinary exception, an infinite loop, normal output, a normal program) were run through the real, unmodified `sandbox-exec`+`echo_sandbox.sb`+`safe_exec_wrapper.py --mode=script` mechanism under three conditions: current inherited-pty stdin, `stdin=subprocess.DEVNULL`, and the real `run_script.py._insert_input_mock()` applied (imported directly, not reimplemented). **New finding, not previously documented anywhere**: `_insert_input_mock()` only patches the `builtins.input` attribute — it does **not** intercept `sys.stdin.readline()`/`sys.stdin.read()`, both of which hang for the full timeout under the mock exactly as under unmodified behavior. `DEVNULL` closes the hang uniformly across all forms tested, but for `readline()`/`read()` specifically it does so by silently returning an empty string and letting the candidate continue to a false `SANDBOX_OK` success — a real false-positive risk Mission 24's original recommendation did not anticipate. Both aliasing patterns (`from builtins import input`, `f = input`) were confirmed correctly caught by the mock regardless, since it patches `builtins.input` before any of the candidate's own code runs. Zero orphaned processes across all 27 real sandbox invocations, including timeout-killed ones. **Contract-mismatch question (Mission 25's own §16-equivalent, "is this one defect or two") is now directly answered**: fixing only the specification leaves F1 with no interactivity check of any kind (traced directly) and the harness exactly as vulnerable to noncompliant generation; fixing only the harness leaves the specification inviting a direction now guaranteed to fail every time, wasting real per-attempt generation compute. These are two manifestations of one unresolved contract mismatch. **Recommendation: spec + harness together (Option D)**, with the harness half specifically needing to *extend* the existing mock pattern to cover `sys.stdin.readline`/`read` rather than switching to `DEVNULL` — not a default "D sounds safest" conclusion, but the option that survived direct adversarial testing of the alternatives. No code was changed; recommendation only, per the mission's explicit investigation-only constraint.

**Update (Mission 27, `audits/2026-09-09_f2_stdin_enforcement_boundary.md`) — the enforcement boundary and exact mechanism are now determined, both refined beyond Mission 26's own tentative framing, via a fuller execution-graph trace and a direct empirical check.** Tracing every real caller of the shared kernel-sandbox mechanism (not just `echo_projects.py` and its one previously-examined sibling) found **six real, live production call paths, plus one confirmed-dead one** — `echo_projects.py`, `self_edit_manager.py` (two distinct modes), `run_script.py`'s two functions serving five further downstream callers (`app/autonomous_loop.py`, `app/core/autonomous_loop_with_optuna.py`, `sandbox/experiment_runner.py`, `app/core/code_verification.py`, `app/core/sandbox_interface.py`), and `app/core/functional_quality.py` (dead, never wired live). None of the eight callers traced individually was found to legitimately need real stdin — six run fixed/known-safe scripts or are textually constrained away from interactivity even without saying so explicitly; the one genuinely novel caveat (`code_verification.py` running code extracted from live chat responses, not just autonomous generation) is a real, previously-unflagged exposure this mission is the first to name. **This shifts the correct enforcement boundary one layer lower than Mission 26 could see**: not `run_script.py` (Option B, reaches only 2 of 6 real callers) but `sandbox/safe_exec_wrapper.py`'s `_install_patches()` — the actual shared execution layer every caller, including `run_script.py` itself, already routes through, and the one file where every other sandbox primitive (write/network/GUI/`ctypes` blocking) already lives, applied unconditionally regardless of `--mode=`. **A direct, safe, unsandboxed empirical check found the mechanism itself is simpler than either Mission 26 candidate**: replacing `sys.stdin` with a small custom stream object (rather than separately patching `builtins.input`, the existing precedent) transparently and correctly blocks `input()` too — because CPython's `input()` internally calls `sys.stdin.readline()` whenever `sys.stdin` is not the interpreter's original object, verified directly, not assumed from documentation. One mechanism, one class, one consistent exception message covers `input()` (direct and both aliasing forms), `read()`, `readline()`, and iteration — strictly more complete than the existing `_insert_input_mock()` and `DEVNULL`, which each needed two separate, still-incomplete mechanisms to approximate the same coverage. **Recommendation: enforce in `safe_exec_wrapper.py`, via `sys.stdin` replacement, not a new execution-mode-selection abstraction** — the codebase already has exactly the kind of caller-selected execution-policy primitive (`--mode=`) Mission's own "Option D" was reaching for, and no real caller was found wanting a different stdin policy under any mode. No code was changed; Mission 28 is specified to implement, not this mission.

**Update (Mission 28, `audits/2026-09-09_f2_stdin_contract_implementation.md`) — implemented, verified through the real sandbox end-to-end, and adversarially attacked; one real gap found and disclosed, not fixed.** `_BlockedStdin` (a minimal `io.TextIOBase` subclass overriding only `read()`/`readline()`/`readlines()`) was added to `sandbox/safe_exec_wrapper.py`'s `_install_patches()`, installed unconditionally. **Exception type was revised from Mission 27's own tentative `RuntimeError` proposal to `PermissionError`**, after checking this file's own existing convention directly: 8/8 other blocked operations in this exact file already use `PermissionError` with a `"[SANDBOX] ..."` prefix; `RuntimeError` belongs to a different file's (`run_script.py`) separate precedent. All 11 required fixtures plus 6 additional ones (unguarded top-level statements under both `--mode=script`/`--mode=import`, a cached-method-reference attack, a stream-surface introspection check, and two direct escape attempts) were run through the real, unmodified `sandbox-exec`+`echo_sandbox.sb`+modified-`safe_exec_wrapper.py` chain — not a bare-Python approximation. `readlines()` and iteration were verified through the real sandbox for the first time (Mission 27 had only inferred these would work by extension). The DEVNULL-style false-success failure mode was directly re-tested and confirmed eliminated. All 8 real production callers Mission 27 traced were regression-tested via direct calls to their actual functions (not a replicated shape) — zero regressions, including one real, pre-existing, unrelated bug correctly identified and *not* misattributed to this change (`hotstove_proof_experience_rep4.py`'s own missing `functools` import). **A real, previously-undiscussed gap was found via adversarial testing and is disclosed, not fixed**: candidate code calling `os.read(0, ...)` directly bypasses `sys.stdin` entirely and still hangs against a live inherited terminal, exactly reproducing Mission 24's original failure — `os.read()` was never among this file's patched primitives, before or after this change. Classified ESCAPES, high severity, flagged as the clearest candidate for a dedicated future mission — not fixed here per the mission's own explicit "do not expand scope beyond what's demonstrated necessary" instruction, since this is a structurally different attack surface (raw fd access) than anything the established generator/harness contract (Missions 25-27) ever scoped. A second, lower-severity escape was also found and disclosed: candidate code can freely reassign `sys.stdin` to its own object, defeating the *contract* but not the *safety property* (the replacement can't return real human input, so it doesn't hang). The subprocess-inheritance question Mission 27 left open is resolved as structurally moot: generated code cannot spawn any subprocess at all, confirmed independently at both the Python level (pre-existing `_blocked()` patches) and the kernel Seatbelt level (`process-fork` already absent from `echo_sandbox.sb`'s allow list) — neither layer needed to be touched. A new, functionally-verified Liveness Ledger check (`f2_stdin_contract`, 51st check) and 5 new discrimination cases were added; the specification's own "an interactive text scenario" wording was also revised (smallest possible change) now that the harness half of Mission 27's "one contract mismatch, not two" finding is concretely closed. `_insert_input_mock()` in `run_script.py` was deliberately left untouched, confirmed to coexist correctly with the new fix as intended defense-in-depth.

**Update (Mission 29, `audits/2026-09-09_os_level_stdin_fd0_forensics.md`) — the `os.read(0, ...)` gap Mission 28 disclosed is now precisely characterized, and the future-fix mechanism itself is pre-vetted, without implementing anything.** Real-sandbox testing (14 fixtures, real pty, the actual production chain) confirmed: `os.read(0, ...)`, `os.fdopen(0, ...)`, `open("/dev/fd/0", ...)`, and `os.dup(0)` then read all hang identically against the real inherited terminal — one root cause (the inherited file descriptor 0 / its open file description), not several independent problems, since all four routes resolve to the same underlying kernel resource. `os.isatty(0)`/`os.ttyname(0)` confirmed fd 0 is a genuine, live tty from inside the sandbox, not merely "a descriptor that happens to exist." A fifth route, `/dev/tty`, was tested and found to fail fast — but a control test with **zero sandboxing at all** reproduced the identical `ENODEV` failure, proving this is incidental subprocess-session plumbing (no controlling terminal ever acquired via `setsid()`/`TIOCSCTTY`), not a security boundary — flagged explicitly so a future mission doesn't mistake this for a working control. `sys.stdin` reassignment (Mission 28's own disclosed low-severity escape) and the raw-fd escape were confirmed independent: reassigning `sys.stdin` does nothing to fd 0 itself, which still hangs when read directly afterward. **Root cause of the remaining gap is NOT the Seatbelt profile**: `echo_sandbox.sb` was read directly and found to already, deliberately, broadly permit the entire `/dev` tree for read (and write-data) — this is correct, necessary-for-stdout/stderr/urandom/null behavior, not an oversight; narrowing it would risk breaking legitimate operation for a problem that isn't actually a kernel-policy gap. **The correct future layer is `safe_exec_wrapper.py` (Option A, same layer as Mission 28's own fix), extended from the Python object to the raw OS descriptor** — because any fix must run before candidate code gets a chance to `os.dup(0)` and preserve access, which only `_install_patches()`'s existing before-`exec_module()` placement guarantees. **A real, pre-emptive semantic warning for whoever builds this fix**: two candidate OS-level mechanisms were tested in complete isolation (a bare `os.fork()`, zero connection to the real sandbox) — redirecting fd 0 to `/dev/null` reproduces Mission 26's already-diagnosed false-success ambiguity (`os.read()` returns `b''`, indistinguishable from legitimate EOF) at the raw-descriptor layer; **closing fd 0 entirely instead raises a genuine, explicit `OSError: [Errno 9] Bad file descriptor`**, satisfying the same explicit-failure contract Mission 28's Python-level fix already established. Recommendation for a future mission: Option A + close-fd-0, not Option A + devnull-redirect — the architecturally "obvious" choice (redirect) would silently reintroduce a defect this project has already fixed once. No code was changed; this mission determined the boundary and mechanism, deliberately without implementing either.

**Update (Mission 30, `audits/2026-09-09_os_level_stdin_fd0_implementation.md`) — implemented and verified through the real sandbox chain; one real, precisely-understood nuance found and disclosed.** `sandbox/safe_exec_wrapper.py`'s `_install_patches()` now closes fd 0 (`try: _os.close(0) except OSError: pass`) immediately after the existing `sys.stdin = _BlockedStdin()` line, placed correctly (confirmed via source review, not assumed) before any candidate code can run and duplicate the original fd. All 5 required attack routes were re-tested through the real, unmodified `sandbox-exec`+`echo_sandbox.sb` chain: `os.read(0, ...)` and `/dev/fd/0` fail immediately with genuine `OSError`s; `os.fdopen(0)`/`os.dup(0)` construction can technically succeed against whatever transiently occupies the fd number, but every real read attempt through either still fails. **A real, initially-surprising finding was traced to full ground truth rather than left unexplained**: `os.close(0)` genuinely, immediately closes the original fd (confirmed as the very first operation), but fd *number* 0 does not stay vacant for the rest of the subprocess's life — CPython's own bytecode-cache write for the candidate's module (a legitimate, permitted, write-mode `open()` inside SCRATCH) gets assigned the just-freed slot 0 by the OS's lowest-available-fd convention, confirmed by finding the real `.pyc` cache artifact on disk and cross-checking `fstat`/`isatty`/`ttyname` against it (regular file, not a tty, `ENOTTY` on `ttyname`) — reproduced 3/3 times. This reoccupation is structurally safe regardless of Python-interpreter specifics: a write-mode-opened fd cannot be read, full stop, so every `os.read()` attempt against it (direct or via `dup()`) fails with `EBADF` in every test performed. The false-success failure mode (Mission 26/29) was explicitly re-tested and confirmed absent at this layer: the conditional checking `data == b""` is never reached, since the read raises before the `if` statement executes. Zero regression across Mission 28's full Python-level contract (all 7 forms re-verified) and all 8 real production callers (tested via direct calls to their actual functions, not a replicated shape) — one apparent anomaly (a fixture that caught its own `OSError` internally, making F2 correctly report success for what looked like an attack attempt) was diagnosed as a test-harness design artifact on the investigator's own part, not a sandbox regression, and disclosed rather than smoothed over. `run.py` does not need a restart for the sandbox mechanism itself — confirmed directly that `safe_exec_wrapper.py` is never `import`ed in-process, only ever invoked as a fresh subprocess reading the file from disk each time; the one genuine in-process consumer (`liveness_ledger.py`'s `f2_stdin_contract` check) was confirmed to already be running pre-Mission-28 code in the live process (its own entry is absent from the live ledger), so this is a pre-existing, not newly-introduced, restart dependency. New Liveness Ledger sub-check added (source-anchor for `_os.close(0)`'s presence, reporting `python_stdin_blocked`/`os_fd0_blocked` independently) rather than a functional subprocess-spawning test, matching this project's own established cost/safety reasoning for checks that run inside the shared production process every 120s.

## R-011 — A clean, hash-frozen N=20 retest of the Tier-5 Council synthesis fixes found real, log-confirmed mechanistic evidence the fixes are working, but was mathematically incapable of resolving the aggregate correctness question the original Tier-4 finding raised.

**Status:** CURRENT. **Confidence:** High for the mechanistic and validity claims (independently re-verified from raw data); explicitly N/A for the aggregate-effect claim (the experiment could not resolve it, not "resolved and negative").

`audits/2026-09-13_tier5_council_correctness_retest.md` (the retest) and `audits/2026-09-14_tier5_retest_adversarial_audit.md` (an independent re-verification of the same raw data, not a second experiment): a fresh, disjoint, hash-frozen 20-task coding suite (`audits/tier5_retest/tier5_retest_task_pool_FROZEN.py`, SHA256 `595bb18a...`) was run paired, treatment (real Tier-5 `detect_full_agreement`/`find_missing_agreed_definitions`) vs. control (same functions monkeypatched to permanent no-ops, reproducing exact pre-refactor behavior). The clean rerun: control 17/20 (85%), treatment 18/20 (90%), +5.0pp, 3 treatment-wins/2 treatment-losses/15 ties, McNemar exact p=1.0000. The adversarial audit independently recomputed every one of these numbers directly from the raw 40-row results file (not from the retest's own summary) and found them accurate, plus found the task-pool hash, driver logic, and both Tier-5 fix functions' live presence in `app/core/river_deliberation.py` all check out. **Two things the adversarial pass established that the original retest report did not**: (1) with only 5 discordant pairs, this experiment was mathematically incapable of reaching p<.05 under any of its own possible outcomes — even a maximal 5-0 split yields p=0.0625 — so "inconclusive" is a structural property of the sample size, not a soft judgment call; (2) direct raw-log evidence (`[DELIBERATION] Synthesis dropped agreed-upon definition(s)... falling back to best candidate`, `tier5_retest_stdout.log`) shows the completeness-check mechanism firing on 3/20 tasks, not the 1/20 the original report credited — a real, disclosed correction that strengthens the mechanistic case.

**What this does and does not establish, stated as precisely as the adversarial audit itself insists on**: real, log-confirmed evidence exists that `find_missing_agreed_definitions()` catches genuine synthesis corruption in production-shaped conditions (mechanistic claim: SUPPORTED).
