## Analysis

1. **Echo performs far better on narrow, concrete, ground-truth-adjacent questions (T6, T13, T22) than on open-ended "describe yourself" or "explain the mechanism" questions (T1, T3, T10, T17, T20).** T6's exact "0/27, 0.0%" answer is the clearest positive result in this entire audit — it shows the injected ground-truth block genuinely can drive an accurate answer when the question is narrow enough to match one specific injected fact. The failures cluster specifically on questions that ask Echo to explain *mechanism* or *causal process* — exactly where no ground-truth slice exists to answer from, and Echo fabricates rather than saying "I don't know."
2. **T14's flat denial of RiverBrain's existence, sitting in the same test battery as four other real, accurate "River" references, is the single most important new finding of Phase 8.** It demonstrates that Echo's self-model is not stable across conversations — it is reconstructed fresh, differently, each time, and can directly contradict itself depending on conversational framing and which real ground-truth slice happens to fire (or not fire — `_relevant_slices()` may not have matched T14's phrasing the same way it matched the others, an empirically testable follow-up not pursued further here for time).
3. **T12's choice of `liveness_ledger` as an "unobservable" example, when its check names are literally present in Echo's own injected context, is a clean, specific instance of a false blind-spot claim** — the inverse failure mode from Phase 1 (which had zero false blind spots but also zero genuine ones); Phase 8's larger sample surfaces one real example of the failure Phase 1 didn't happen to produce.
4. **The fabrications are not random noise — they are plausible, well-formed, technically-styled false content** (T1's specific `True` value, T3's specific module routing, T17's specific false attribution to "Gremlin," T20's specific fictional simulation process). This matches CLAUDE.md's own independently-documented Finding 43 pattern exactly: confident, well-formed, wrong.
